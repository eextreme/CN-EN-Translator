That night, Kui Xin got a proper rest.

By the time she woke up it was nearly ten. She'd slept in, which she hadn't done in a very long time.

In her final year of high school she'd been up a little after five every morning, and after that her body clock had set itself so firmly that even during summer break she never failed to wake before seven. Once she started playing “Crimson Earth,” though, Kui Xin's schedule had descended into a chaos that could have given her a heart attack at any moment.

With her overstuffed brain finally given a chance to unwind, Kui Xin woke up with the energy to think things through carefully and lay out what she had to do next in some kind of logical order.

At the moment, Kui Xin had three urgent problems to deal with.

First, the Popov family's ransom.

Second, screening new members for the Sea Without Light and assigning them positions and tasks.

Third, pinning down Depriver 777's location and identity.

The first two were manageable.

As for the Popov family's ransom, Kui Xin had decided to follow Adam's advice and negotiate with Polina in private. If Polina took the way out she was offering and Kui Xin got the ransom without a hitch, the matter would be settled.

As for new members for the Sea Without Light, Kui Xin had already used the forum to screen for reliable candidates during her latest trip back to the First World. Lancet, Golden Ring, Wei Li, Samanto and a handful of others were all on the shortlist.

The last problem was the most important one, but Kui Xin would only have her hands free to deal with it once the first two were solved.

She and Augus were a thousand li apart. Floating Port City was inland, much too far from White Whale City to reach in a hurry. Augus wasn't a tree stump; he wasn't going to stay put. So Kui Xin had to free herself up as quickly as possible, before Augus moved somewhere else and became hard to track.

Augus's Second World body was most likely inside the nuclear power plant in Floating Port City, which was a Dark Realm. Kui Xin would rather he died in there, but in a sense... she also didn't want him to die in there. She wanted Augus to come out.

If Augus came out, it would mean there was still a way in and out of a Dark Realm, that it wasn't a one-way trip. However Augus managed to escape would be an extremely valuable reference for Kui Xin. To her, it was crucial.

Besides those three problems, there were also a handful of odds and ends Kui Xin had to consider.

Three great mountains still weighed down on Kui Xin: Mechanical Dawn, the Resistance and the Special Intelligence Division.

The threat they posed had never gone away.

But even though those threats were right on her doorstep, Kui Xin was no longer the helpless ant she had been at the start, there for anyone to crush. She now had enough clout to stand up to these big organizations. She couldn't take them on head-on, but she could use all sorts of abilities to outmaneuver them.

Kui Xin's advantage was mobility. If she couldn't win, she could always retreat. She would forever be the snake hiding in the shadows, while her enemies stood out in the open.

Mechanical Dawn had lost Four Leaves, and Trailblaze Technologies had been hacked by Kui Xin. However confident Eve might be, she would have to move her bases, so for the time being she wouldn't have the means to come after Kui Xin.

As for the Resistance, Kui Xin had driven off the possessing Ghost in the Battle of Zero and then turned Amber to her side, so she had more or less weathered that crisis.

With the Special Intelligence Division, Li Wanran was covering for her and Adam was helping her too. As long as Kui Xin didn't come face to face with the Division's S-rank Awakened, they had no way to touch her.

After she sorted through her thoughts, the situation became much clearer in her mind. The various factions were tangled together and Kui Xin had made plenty of enemies, but things were still under control.

She let herself relax a little, got up, washed, had something to eat, then did a few sets of stretches to loosen up.

It had snowed again in White Whale City overnight. When Kui Xin left the base, fully dressed and geared up, she saw grimy snow piled along both sides of the road. The temperature had risen a bit that morning and the snow was half-melted, leaving the roads in terrible shape.

Kui Xin turned left, then right, then left again, making her way into the alley like she'd done it a hundred times. There she opened a spatial vortex and stepped through it to the base where Su Rong was staying.

Su Rong had been up for a while and was working out on an indoor treadmill. She was so happy to see Kui Xin that she nearly fell off it.

"You're here!" Su Rong jumped off the treadmill. "Are we doing anything today? Are we going out recruiting again?"

"That's not why I came," Kui Xin said. "How are things outside the Dark Realm? Has anything changed?"

"No, everything's the same. That night was really quiet. The players all stayed on the outer edge, and there was nobody at all in the inner zone," Su Rong said. "When I saw what you did, I was terrified. I ran into White Dove near the tobacco factory, and since White Dove knows you, I thought I'd ask him about the Dark Realm. Then I heard a woman with him talking to him, and they mentioned your codename, so I had my shadow show itself and talk to them."

"Talk how? Your shadow can't speak. Did you borrow their phone and type?" Kui Xin asked.

"Yeah, you guessed it," Su Rong said sheepishly. "The woman's codename is Dusk, and she seemed really anxious. She and White Dove both guessed you'd gone into the Dark Realm. She wanted to go in after you too, but White Dove stopped her. He said they had to wait and see whether you could come back out of the Dark Realm first. Only once they were sure you couldn't would they think about what to do next."

Kui Xin asked, "What did you tell them?"

"I didn't know what I should or shouldn't say, so I only said I was Ash from the Sea Without Light and Fer-de-lance's teammate," Su Rong said. "I didn't dare say anything else. After you went in that night, the three of us kept watch outside, waiting for you to come out."

She asked anxiously, "Can you still get out? Fer-de-lance?"

"I can. I definitely can. I just don't know how yet," Kui Xin said quietly.

"Do you need food? If you can't get out any time soon, maybe we could drop food into the Dark Realm," Su Rong said. "Do you need medicine? Or anything else? We'll get it ready before the next return. We can pack everything up and throw it in, and maybe you'll be able to pick it up."

Kui Xin thought it over. "Sure. I need a lot of medicine. Get some food ready too."

"You're hurt?" Su Rong caught the important part.

"Mm." Kui Xin nodded. "Sorry to trouble those of you still on the outside, but please prepare as much as you can."

Su Rong bit her lip and looked at Kui Xin, sounding a little miserable. "I hate how useless I am. Sometimes I wonder if I should give some things up and go do what I ought to be doing..."

Kui Xin had told Su Rong she'd taken a leave of absence from school, but she hadn't expected Su Rong to start thinking about dropping her studies too.

"Don't think like that. A normal life shouldn't be something that holds you back," Kui Xin said patiently.

Looking at Su Rong's unhappy face, she went on, "Of course you can give something up to do what you think is right. But that decision should come from your own heart, not because someone else told you to. I'm not recommending you give anything up because I don't think your decision is mature enough yet. You haven't thought through the consequences. If you think it all through and feel you can handle the consequences, then of course you can go ahead."

Su Rong nodded silently.

"I might need you for something today, so stay on standby at the base." Kui Xin checked the time. "I've got other things to take care of first. See you."

A spatial vortex yawned open behind her, and Su Rong watched her step into it.

He Kangshi was sitting in the bar supervising the work. The bar hadn't opened yet, but the renovations should be done by the end of the day. Small-time thugs from the local gangs had been drafted as temporary builders, and they were working surprisingly fast.

Four Leaves was the first to notice Kui Xin. He had been sitting on the floor stacking beer cans like building blocks when he suddenly seemed to sense something and turned around, just in time to see the spatial vortex open.

"Fer-de-lance!" he shouted, his eyes shining.

Kui Xin was taken aback. His enthusiasm and excitement left her not quite sure what to do with herself, so she just said, "Good morning, Four Leaves."

To her surprise, Four Leaves politely answered, "Good morning."

It seemed he wasn't quite so clueless about social niceties anymore.

"I taught him that." He Kangshi came over, rubbing his head. "I've been like a kindergarten teacher these last few days. I have to teach him table manners, and even how to deal with people. But Four Leaves is really obedient. He listens to everything I say."

"Oh, thanks for your hard work," Kui Xin said offhandedly. "I just came by to check on the renovations. Keep an eye on things."

"Will do!" He Kangshi said briskly.

He sidled up to Kui Xin and lowered his voice. "Why was Ash so worked up last night? Can you tell me, sis?"

"It's nothing big. Something happened a while ago and she couldn't reach me." After explaining, Kui Xin looked him over. "You feel a bit different. Like you've gotten a little stronger."

"You can tell?" He Kangshi said in surprise. "I leveled up. I'm C-rank now. I'm still pretty much a scrub in a fight, but at least I can actually use my ability in combat."

He Kangshi had a mind-type ability, similar to one of Augus's abilities, called Telekinetic Control. Basically, it let him move objects without touching them. But because his ability was so low-level, and he'd never had any real fights to hone it in, his proficiency wasn't great. If he ran into an enemy, he'd be better off just using a gun.

Still a bit too low... Kui Xin thought.

The Sea Without Light's biggest weakness was that the people Kui Xin trusted were all too low-ranked to hold their own. The organization couldn't survive without her protection, and that tied her hands to some extent.

This wasn't something that could be fixed overnight, so Kui Xin had to set it aside for now.

"Get back to work. I'm heading out." The moment Kui Xin turned around, someone tugged at the hem of her clothes.

"You only said... one sentence to me, and it was only four words," Four Leaves stressed earnestly. "You were gone for a long, long, long time, and after you came here you only stayed for..." He held up his fingers and clumsily gestured. "Only a teeny tiny bit of time!"

"So how many sentences do you want me to say to you, and how many words?" Kui Xin turned her head to ask him.

Four Leaves looked lost for a moment, then said firmly, "Lots and lots of sentences! Lots and lots of words!"

"Then save up all those sentences and words, and I'll say them to you later." Kui Xin took his hand and pulled the hem of her clothes free. "Right now, I have to say 'goodbye' to you first."

Four Leaves watched forlornly as Kui Xin left.

He Kangshi said to Four Leaves, "Cheer up, little guy. Big Sis Fer-de-lance has already been really gentle with you. She's not the type who likes to joke around! She brought you into this world, and she takes her responsibility for you pretty seriously. She's just way too busy!"

"Oh..." Four Leaves said blankly.

...

"You're here." Amber nodded to Kui Xin. "We got it out of him. This is Polina's private number."

Kui Xin made a note of the number. Sitting on the sofa, she turned to look at Abra, whose injuries still hadn't fully healed. "If I negotiate with Polina privately, do you think she'll agree to my terms?"

"Hard to say," Abra said cautiously. "You can threaten her with the family's reputation and negotiate on that basis, but threatening her through family ties won't get you far."

Kui Xin took out her communicator, toyed with it in her hand for a while, then entered the number on the screen.

Before dialing, Kui Xin jerked her chin at Amber, and Amber, understanding at once, taped Abra's mouth shut.

"Beep—"

The call connected. Faint breathing came through the receiver, and then an elegant, composed female voice said, "Is that you, Abra? Or is this someone from the Sea Without Light?"

"You really are a perceptive woman, Madam Polina." Kui Xin's voice, run through a voice changer, went into the receiver.

"Only family members call this number, and I have all of their numbers on record. This one is unfamiliar," Polina said coldly. "My guess is that my useless younger brother Abra gave you the number."
