"Careful. The line is encrypted, but if you talk to her for too long, she may be able to trace it back," Adam's voice said in Kui Xin's hidden earpiece. "I'm fairly confident in my data-processing abilities, but it's better to be safe."

Kui Xin didn't answer Adam. Instead she asked Polina, "Ms. Polina, since you've guessed who I am, why not have a guess at why I'm calling?"

"Money." Every word Polina spoke landed with the crisp finality of a hammer driving a nail. She was used to giving orders, and her tone carried an unmistakable note that brooked no argument.

"You guessed right, Ms. Polina," Kui Xin said unhurriedly. "And since you've guessed, let's not waste each other's time. Let's make a deal."

Polina said, "Before we make any deal, please answer one question for me."

"Go ahead," Kui Xin said politely.

"Why has the Sea Without Light gone after the Popov family?" Polina asked. "You must have picked your target deliberately, not at random. I'm curious. Did our family, or some member of our family, offend you somehow?"

"That's a good question."

Polina heard a hoarse laugh come through the receiver, and then the voice said, "All I can tell you is that you weren't very lucky. You happened to wander right into our sights."

It was an answer that answered nothing.

Polina shifted her gaze slightly and raised her eyelids. The office door slid open without a sound, and a technician from the Special Intelligence Division walked in. He set a metal case gently on the floor, took out a cable and connected it to Polina's line.

The technician gestured to Polina, signalling her to keep stalling.

Without letting anything show, Polina spoke into the microphone again. "That's it? That answer is far too perfunctory."

"That's my answer," the other party said. "Does Ms. Polina have any other questions?"

"Are you the leader of the Sea Without Light, 'Black Snake'?" Polina said.

"No." The voice paused for a moment. "I haven't even introduced myself yet. How rude of me. My codename is Fer-de-lance, and I handle the organization's external affairs."

"All right, Fer-de-lance." Polina looked over at the Special Intelligence Division technician, who signalled her to keep stalling. So short a time wasn't enough for him to trace the source of the signal.

So she asked, "Tell me, is my son still alive?"

The Sea Without Light member called "Fer-de-lance" was silent for a few seconds, then abruptly hung up.

"Call disconnected," an automated prompt announced from the receiver.

Polina frowned. The call had ended without the slightest warning.

"Did you trace it?" she asked.

The technician replied, "No... The Sea Without Light's technical skills are definitely no joke. I followed the signal to somewhere outside White Whale City, went around in a huge circle and still couldn't find the source. Maybe there wasn't enough time."

Polina said with certainty, "They'll call again."

Sure enough, in less than a minute another call came in from an unknown number.

Polina answered and heard the familiar voice again. Fer-de-lance said, "I'm surprised you actually bothered to ask about your son."

"He is my son, after all," Polina said coldly.

"Spare me the hypocrisy, Polina. I know you don't care about your family," Fer-de-lance said. "We'd better get straight to the point."

Out of the corner of her eye, Polina glanced at the technician. His brows were knitted tightly. The caller had switched to a different encrypted line, and he had to start tracing the source all over again.

"We're an organization that keeps its promises. You pay, and we stop dumping your dirt. We'll do exactly what we say."

Polina stalled for time. "I thought money wasn't what you were after. I thought you wanted something else. After all, nothing you've done so far looks like a simple demand for cash."

"Money is a wonderful thing. Everyone wants it, and it can buy most things in this world," Fer-de-lance said. "Enough nonsense, Ms. Polina. I know you're trying to stall so you can pin down my location. All I need from you is an answer: yes or no."

For once, Polina felt genuinely angry. There were very few things in the world she cared about, but the family's reputation and its face were among them. She couldn't ignore those. After two kidnappings in a row, the Popov family had become a laughingstock in high society!

"You want twenty billion?" Polina took a deep breath and did her best to stay calm.

Fer-de-lance: "Yes."

Polina said, "Once you have the twenty billion, you'll stop going after the Popov family?"

Fer-de-lance said, "Yes."

"And if I refuse?" Polina said.

"Then the Sea Without Light won't mind making you the laughingstock of the whole plutocrat elite all over again. Believe me, we can do it." Fer-de-lance laughed. "You don't want a third kidnapping, Polina. Twenty billion for peace of mind is a bargain."

"And afterwards, the smear campaigns and the death threats will all stop?" Polina said.

Fer-de-lance said, "Of course. When we make a promise, we keep it."

"So the Popov family is supposed to spend twenty billion on a flimsy promise you could break whenever you like." Polina very nearly laughed out loud in contempt.

"If you don't want to, we'll just keep wearing you down." With that, Fer-de-lance read out a string of numbers. "Take your time to think it over, Polina. Remember our account number. When you've made up your mind, transfer the money. Once it arrives, this ends."

"Beep—"

The call ended. They had hung up right on time.

Polina looked at the technician with a grim expression. When he shook his head at her, her expression grew even darker.

Money! Money! Money!

Every other word out of the Sea Without Light was about money. But if they were really short of money, why hadn't they taken a single coin when they kidnapped Abra? Did they think it was small change? Yet the assets in Abra's private residence were worth several billion altogether. That was hardly a small sum.

Polina couldn't shake the feeling that they weren't purely after money. And yet the Sea Without Light had chosen this exact moment to propose a truce, and the condition for that truce was money. Her resolve wavered, and she could no longer tell what they really wanted.

Most importantly, Polina feared that even paying the twenty billion wouldn't make the Sea Without Light stop. She suspected it was a trap.

Twenty billion wasn't much. Any company under the Popov family's umbrella had a market value of at least twenty billion, and Polina's personal fortune was far larger than that.

She quickly weighed things up in her head.

If she paid, she would get the Sea Without Light's promise of a truce. If she didn't, they would keep targeting the Popov family; Fer-de-lance had even threatened as much outright. Another family member would probably be kidnapped, followed by a long string of scandals. The Popovs couldn't all hide in their mansions like turtles pulling their heads into their shells. And even if they did, the Sea Without Light could come to their doors and take them anyway. Abra had been minding his own business in his own private residence, and he'd still been kidnapped.

The promised truce might be a lie, but Polina had seen enough to know the Special Intelligence Division couldn't do anything about the Sea Without Light anytime soon.

Trace them technically? The Sea Without Light had formidable network skills of its own.

Take them by force? The Special Intelligence Division didn't even know where their base was.

Polina rubbed her forehead. The irritation on her face vanished in an instant, and she said graciously to the technician from the Special Intelligence Division, "Sorry to have dragged you out here on such short notice."

"It's a shame we couldn't get anything useful," the technician said regretfully. "I recorded the audio and will take it back for analysis. We'll also keep tracking the account they gave you. If any member of the Sea Without Light contacts you again, please let us know right away."

Polina kept up her smiling mask as she watched him leave.

The moment the technician was gone, Polina contacted her private secretary.

"Withdraw twenty billion from the family bank and transfer it to this number..." Polina read out the account number.

The secretary quickly did as she instructed.

Polina pulled loose the bow tie of her formal suit and leaned back in her chair, idly tapping her fingers on the armrest.

She looked calm on the outside, but inside she was seething.

She was used to threatening other people. Even at the centre of the family's power struggles, no one had ever had her so completely under their thumb. This was the first time she had been entirely at someone else's mercy from start to finish.

Polina had always believed she had no weak spots. She had none of those soft feelings, very few people or things could tie her down, and her head was always clear. She always knew what she wanted. Only after this incident did Polina clearly realize that she had a weakness after all...

Her weakness was her family.

Her glory came from the family. Her foundation came from the family. Everything she had gained depended on the backing of the Popov family as a whole. So the family couldn't fall, and she couldn't let it become something people gossiped about and mocked.

She felt humiliated, more humiliated than she had ever been.

Polina closed her eyes and waited for the throbbing in her temples to settle.

Then she heard a message alert. She opened her eyes and saw that the message wasn't from her secretary. It was from Fer-de-lance.

A few lines of text appeared on the office's central system interface: "As you wish: a truce. But I'm sorry, Dmitri is already dead. I imagine you'd already guessed that. Abra, however, is still alive, and we will return him to the Popov family."

Return him? How would they return him?

Polina instinctively began to think about whether there was any room to work with here. For example, they could arrange an on-site hostage exchange, and maybe she could team up with the Special Intelligence Division to capture members of the Sea Without Light...

She lowered her eyes and thought for a moment, then contacted the Special Intelligence Division and the Investigation Department to ask for armed support.

But the maddening day wasn't over yet. Half an hour later, her secretary suddenly put through an emergency call.

"Chairwoman, look outside!" The secretary was so flustered she could barely get the words out.

Polina looked up without thinking and turned to the floor-to-ceiling windows of her office.

Her office sat high up in the skyscraper, and the wide view showed her everything outside. In the sky straight ahead of her window, a garishly decorated advertising airship was drifting slowly toward the building.

Polina looked closer and saw a long rope hanging from the bottom of the airship. Someone was tied to it, and that someone was Abra Popov.

Abra dangled from the rope, his face deathly pale. That high up, he didn't dare move a muscle and could only let the airship carry him slowly along. He swayed back and forth in the wind as if he were on a swing, looking both pathetic and ridiculous.

Worse still, the airship was dropping flyers as it flew, and the words on the electronic sign hanging from it kept changing.

The electronic sign read: "I, Abra Popov, am a traitor to the plutocrats."

The colorful letters changed, and another line appeared in fancy script.

"I am a member of the Resistance."

The secretary's voice shook. "The airship keeps dropping flyers, and they say the same thing as the electronic sign..."

Polina's vision went dark, and she nearly lost her footing.

In that instant she saw how terrible the consequences would be.

Polina knew all about the Resistance. It was the Federation's political red line. Anyone who got mixed up with it was doomed, and anyone who touched it became a top target for a crackdown. Whether or not Abra was actually a member of the Resistance, the Popov family was now in serious trouble, far worse than having family members kidnapped.
