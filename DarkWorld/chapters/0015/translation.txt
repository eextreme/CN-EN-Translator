Kui Xin had looked up Floating Port City online ahead of time, so she knew what kind of city it was.

Floating Port City sat right next to the Federal Administrative Center, and in both economic and political standing it was without question at the very top of the pyramid.

"Right now we're… outside Wuli City." Kui Xin opened her wristband to check the coordinates. "Still over two thousand kilometers from Floating Port City… Tch, that's far… Let's rest first."

"Does an S-rank spatial vortex take that much out of you?" Amber asked.

"Yeah, a lot." Kui Xin let out a breath. "If I open one vortex, a short rest is enough. If I open several in a row, I really start to feel it. How much stamina it costs depends on the distance. The farther I go, the more it takes. Short hops barely cost anything, of course."

"That makes sense… Every vortex you open covers almost four hundred kilometers," Amber said. "If it were Night Cicada, he'd have collapsed long ago after using his ability that many times in one day."

If Kui Xin hadn't sent Lancet and the others to Black Sea City, she and Amber could have gone much farther. Unfortunately, a powerful ability always came at a steep cost. After opening a few too many vortexes, Night Cicada used to need an oxygen tank to breathe. Kui Xin was in far better shape than he had been. At the very least, she still had something left in reserve.

Getting from White Whale City to Black Sea City had taken her six spatial vortexes. Getting from Black Sea City to Floating Port City would take an estimated twelve.

White Whale City actually sat fairly central, roughly as far from Floating Port City as it was from Black Sea City. The problem was that Black Sea City was a long way from the Federal Administrative Center. One was on the coast, and the other lay in the heart of the continent.

"It's already half past eleven. Lunchtime." Kui Xin dropped onto the ground, took the food she'd packed out of her gear bag and bit into it.

Amber sat down too and took out his own food.

"I figure I'll need six hours to recover," Kui Xin said.

"All right. I'll keep watch," Amber said. "You get some rest."

Once she had eaten and had something to drink, Kui Xin leaned against a large rock by the roadside and closed her eyes.

The noon sun was fierce. They had sat down in the rock's shade, but as time went on the sun slowly came around, and the harsh light was about to reach Kui Xin's face.

Amber couldn't tell whether she was actually asleep, and he didn't wake her. Instead he took off his backpack, moved over to face the sun, and held the pack up over his head to keep the sun off her.

A few hours later, the sunlight had lost its heat.

Amber lowered the backpack from over his head, and Kui Xin finished her rest.

"Let's keep going." When she stretched, her bones gave a loud series of cracks.

The spatial vortex opened again, and they headed for the next coordinate point they had mapped out, working their way toward Floating Port City step by step.

……

Floating Port City's skyline had a completely different look from the cities they had seen so far.

"Finally, a city with a subway…" Kui Xin muttered to herself. "Neither Black Sea City nor White Whale City had a subway, just maglev rail cars. White Whale City didn't even have those."

"Transit here is split into an aboveground part and an underground part," Amber said. "There are maglev lines and underrail lines. I came here once, a long time ago."

"In your world it's called a subway? Here we call it the 'underrail.' It means about the same thing," Adam said. "Different cities use different kinds of transit, depending on their geography."

Many of the Federation's cities had long histories. When some of them were first built, nobody had planned for future growth, so their transit networks were incomplete. Part of the reason was also that back then, Federation technology wasn't yet good enough to ignore the terrain.

Black Sea City had no underground transit because it was a coastal city with heavy rain year after year. A subway there would flood easily, and if the drainage couldn't keep up, things would go wrong. So Black Sea City took a different route and built maglev tracks that ran across the whole city.

White Whale City had started out as a mining town. The soil there was unusual, and the city sat near the pole. Digging through the permafrost was hard, so underground transit would have cost far too much. Maglev rail cars didn't work either. It was winter there for most of the year, so ice would build up on the tracks, and clearing it off was expensive too. That was why White Whale City's roads were so wide and its overpasses were stacked layer upon layer. They took pressure off the city's traffic.

With the Federation's current technology, it was possible to force aboveground and underground transit systems into coastal cities and polar regions. But rebuilding a city like that cost too much. The existing routes were overloaded, but they still more or less worked, so why build a whole new system? And so the plans kept getting pushed back.

"Floating Port City is the Federation's most important transit hub," Adam said. "From here you can reach the heart of the Federation as well as other cities. If you look at a map, you'll see that the city at the center of the whole web of transit routes is Floating Port City."

"Why isn't the Federal Administrative Center in the middle?" Kui Xin asked, connected to Adam through Data Control.

"Because Floating Port City came before the Federal Administrative Center," Adam explained. "The Federal Administrative Center was built later. Floating Port City was originally supposed to become the administrative center, but it had too many factories and not enough room to build. So a new city was developed next to Floating Port City. That's where the Federal Administrative Center came from."

"The history of these cities would make a great research topic…" Kui Xin said. "If a sociologist ever crossed over into the Second World, they might write a few papers. Something like 'A Comparative Study of Urban Development in the First and Second Worlds' or 'On the Influence of Geography on Urban Character in the Second World'…"

"It's certainly an academic question worth exploring," Adam agreed. "Too bad papers like that could never be published."

"Come on, let's take the subway… the underrail," Kui Xin said to Amber.

Using the spatial vortex over and over had worn her out, so if she could ride instead of walking, she'd save a bit of energy.

"We don't have legal IDs. How are we supposed to ride it?" Amber said. "The second we walk in, the Investigation Department's transit surveillance system will scan us."

"That's not a problem," Kui Xin said lightly.

Amber nodded thoughtfully. "All right, if you say so."

The city's transit system was run by the Investigation Department, and the Investigation Department answered to Adam. Kui Xin could go anywhere she liked in this city without worrying about the Investigation Department coming after her.

Even though Adam could slip them through a back door, they still had to be discreet about it. After all, Eve was also watching her from the shadows.

There weren't many underrail entrances on the outskirts of Floating Port City. Kui Xin and Amber changed into ordinary clothes and put on lifelike human-skin masks they had ordered at a steep price from a black-market doctor. With backpacks on, they passed themselves off as travelers.

They got through the underrail security check and ID scan without a hitch and boarded the train without even paying for tickets.

Sitting in a clean, roomy underrail seat, Kui Xin suddenly felt as if she were back home in the First World.

Through the glass she could see flashy ads playing on the walls all around the platform. The colorful ads were reflected in the train windows, and so were the passengers. At a glance, everyone in the train, standing or sitting, was tinted in gorgeous colors by the glowing ads.

The train had several cars, and only a few people were on board. As it pulled away, the hanging handles swayed back and forth. The train rushed through a silver tunnel, and at that speed the ad walls on either side blurred into streaks of colored light. Kui Xin felt as if she were passing through a tunnel full of auroras, with deep blue and deep purple light flowing over her.

"It's beautiful," Kui Xin said softly.

"It is," Amber said. "The cities people build are all beautiful. But not every part of every city is equally beautiful."

The beautiful grew more beautiful and the ugly grew uglier. The developed grew more developed and the backward fell further behind.

Two hours later, the underrail train reached Kui Xin's destination: the slums of Floating Port City.

Even a city this glamorous still had slums. That was why Amber had said not every part of a city was equally beautiful. In the places the light never reached, people lived like rats.

When they came up through the underrail exit, they found that the up escalator had broken down long ago. The "Out of Service, Awaiting Repair" sign was coated in dust, as if it had been sitting there for quite a while.

Kui Xin and Amber had no choice but to climb out on foot.

After a few steps aboveground, they rounded a tall building, and all they could see ahead were low gray houses. Faint lights glowed from those little gray boxes. This was where the poor lived.

Kui Xin gazed into the distance. Her eyes passed over the little gray boxes and locked onto a cluster of towering buildings far away.

A nuclear power plant!

The plant was impossible to miss. In the dark, the sheet-metal shells of its buildings gave off a cold, eerie gleam. Kui Xin could also see the "domes." The containment domes, built to prevent a nuclear leak, sealed several of the plant's building complexes tightly inside. They were so huge that even from that far away she could feel their oppressive weight.

The plant's buildings were far larger than anything in the slums, like a giant looking down at ants. Next to the nuclear plant, the little gray houses of the slums were like pebbles at the foot of a mountain range.

"How big are those containment domes?" Kui Xin asked gravely.

"Each one covers about the area of twenty soccer fields, and there are two of them. Because they're so enormous, they're held up by internal support frames, a bit like a tent," Adam said. "Together, the two domes cover nearly thirty hectares."

"Damn," Kui Xin said. "Living here is like having Death for a next-door neighbor…"

"The nuclear leak back then was contained quickly, and it had very little effect on the surrounding area," Adam said. "It's just that nobody knows whether the problems it left behind will blow up someday."

"It's like Death is holding a blade to your throat every second of every day, and you never know when it's going to come down," Kui Xin said.

She pulled her gaze back and looked at Amber.

"Amber, this next part is your area of expertise," Kui Xin said.

Maybe he was imagining it, but Amber could have sworn he saw expectation and encouragement in Kui Xin's eyes.

He said, "Don't look at me like that… That look is making me question my 'area of expertise.'"

"Why would you question it?" Kui Xin said. "It's not like there are any sewers at the nuclear plant for you to crawl through this time."

"Fine, I get it." Amber sighed weakly. "I'll take control of a few small animals and send them over to scope things out… That's the whole reason you brought me along, isn't it?"

"Split them into two groups," Kui Xin said. "One goes into the nuclear plant to see what's going on, and the other searches the surrounding area and helps me find someone."

She raised her communicator and sent Amber a picture. Depriver 777's face appeared on the screen.
