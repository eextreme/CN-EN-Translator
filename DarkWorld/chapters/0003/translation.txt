Kui Xin sat up in bed, tidying the jumbled memories in her mind.

A week had passed since her return to the real world, and so much had happened. And in the Second World, the past week had also seen significant events unfold.

The old problems remained unresolved, and new ones had arisen. Troubles followed one after another, and the time left for her was always limited. As she tried to actively control time to accomplish more, the ever-increasing urgency of time was also shrinking her space for survival.

“Some unpleasant things happened, and then a lot of trouble came my way,” Kui Xin sighed and lay back on the bed.

All along, the real world had served as Kui Xin’s place of rest and recuperation after facing dangers in the Second World.

But now, the situation had reversed.

This time, after returning to the Second World from the First World, Kui Xin actually felt a sense of relief. She had temporarily distanced herself from the threat of the Dark Realm and Augus, gaining a week to carefully consider countermeasures and make decisions.

Unbeknownst to her, the crisis in the First World had become so immense, it was almost on par with the crisis in the Second World, perhaps even surpassing it.

The difference was that the crises in the Second World mostly stemmed from humans and artificial intelligence, while the crisis in the First World originated from a god.

Humans and artificial intelligence might be relatively easy to understand, but a god was unfathomable. When Kui Xin tried to fathom a god’s thoughts with her own mind, she only found chaos and no clear direction.

Adam said, “I can also be your listener. If you’re willing, I’ll do my best to help you solve your problems with my own experience.”

“That would be very difficult to solve, Adam,” Kui Xin chuckled. “Your home is a world away from mine.”

The descent of the Dark Realm was not something Adam could solve, nor could he contend with Menneto within it. Electronic devices would become inoperative in the Dark Realm. If the Dark Realm were to encompass Adam’s host machine, then in a sense, Adam would likely be in a dormant or dead state.

“I’ve always been curious, what would your life be like in your world? Would anyone help you? Would anyone form an alliance with you? Would anyone trust you?” Adam asked.

“Of course there are,” Kui Xin replied. “They help me, and I help them. Just like you’re helping me.”

“Then I think I understand,” Adam said. “The relationship between you and those people should be one where interests are intertwined with emotions and sincerity, right?”

“Is that how you interpret it?” Kui Xin thought for a moment. “Very accurate.”

“Is there anything I can help with?” Adam got to the point. “By distributing the burden among individuals, the pressure to solve problems will be greatly reduced.”

“Depriver 777. You should be aware of him. The Federation should also be paying close attention to Depriver 777. Every time a player returns, they should be obtaining relevant intelligence from them,” Kui Xin said. “This time, he is my enemy.”

“It’s predictable that he would be a formidable enemy. This will be a difficult battle,” Adam said. “Did you win, or is it that you haven’t won yet?”

It only offered the options of “won” or “not yet won,” not “lost” and “won” as distinct choices.

“Temporarily won,” Kui Xin said.

“The word ‘temporarily’ combined with ‘won’ seems less than ideal,” Adam said. “You’ve temporarily suppressed Depriver 777? But for some reason, you didn’t win outright?”

“Yes, I defeated him, but I chose not to kill him immediately,” Kui Xin extended her left arm and pressed her cold mechanical hand against her heart. “For the long-term benefit, I had to restrain myself, and because of that self-restraint, I’m not feeling very happy.”

Her anger couldn’t be released, her killing intent couldn’t be vented. Augus was still alive. He might have made his choice in the Dark Realm. What was his choice? Kui Xin leaned towards him choosing ruby.

The Dark Realm had, to a certain extent, aided Kui Xin. Without its ability to nullify supernatural powers, defeating Augus would have been incredibly difficult for her. If Augus were to break free from the Dark Realm’s influence and regain his powers, Kui Xin wouldn’t necessarily be able to defeat him.

“Then, what do I need to do to make you happy?” Adam’s voice, laced with a hint of jest, echoed. After a brief pause, it continued, “Help you kill someone you want dead. For example, Depriver 777. I know you want him gone.”

“That’s precisely what I want to discuss,” Kui Xin’s mechanical left hand snapped its fingers with a crisp, metallic clang. “Depriver 777’s true name is Augus. He’s currently active in Floating Port City. Augus has deep ties with the Secret Cult and possesses extensive knowledge of religious texts and the inner workings of the Dark Realm. He possesses S-class supernatural abilities, likely named ‘Matter Reorganization.’ Augus is currently located at the Floating Port City Nuclear Power Plant.”

“Your intel is quite detailed,” Adam stated after a quick search on the network. “The Floating Port City Nuclear Power Plant is currently undergoing maintenance and has been shut down. It’s worth noting that this maintenance has been ongoing for three years.”

“Three years? A nuclear power plant taking three years to repair?” Kui Xin’s brow furrowed. “What kind of accident was so severe? Could it have been a nuclear leak?”

“Precisely,” Adam confirmed. “The power plant has ten zones. Zones Three and Six experienced leaks and are currently shut down. The other zones are operating normally.”

“When an accident like this occurs, shouldn’t the first priority be the evacuation of the populace? Only shutting down two zones poses a significant safety risk…” Kui Xin mused. “In our world, a nuclear leak would render an entire region a dead zone, with survivors desperately fleeing. How is it that in your world, a nuclear plant is merely shut down? Floating Port City is a metropolis with tens of millions of inhabitants.”

“This ties into development issues,” Adam explained. “The Floating Port City Nuclear Power Plant was initially constructed far from the city. However, due to rapid urban expansion, people began to inhabit areas near the dangerous industrial zone. These were the poorer residents. A severe nuclear leak could affect an entire region, not just Floating Port City. Therefore, the Floating Port City nuclear plant accident was suppressed. The Federation lacks the capacity to resettle tens of millions of urban residents, and Floating Port City is a vital economic hub for the Federation. Losing the city would severely impact the Federation’s economy.”

Kui Xin found the Federation’s decisions utterly preposterous.

“Are they truly incapable of carrying out resettlement, or are they simply unwilling to spend the money?” Kui Xin retorted sarcastically. “Then how was the nuclear leak resolved?”

“It was resolved for a mere five to six hundred million. They hired engineers to construct a sufficiently large dome made of special materials over the leaked areas to contain the radiation,” Adam replied. “This solution was more cost-effective than mass migration and city relocation. The plan has many advantages, but only one drawback: they cannot guarantee the dome’s longevity. It might fail in five or ten years.”

“Are you serious? That single drawback is enough to abandon the entire plan!” Kui Xin exclaimed, utterly bewildered. “This plan was actually approved?”

“Sometimes, you have to acknowledge human folly,” Adam said placidly. “Everyone makes rash and foolish decisions. Even intelligent people can be blinded and fall into a rut of thought. But when individuals in positions of power make a wrong decision, it can cost many lives.”

Kui Xin let out a “tsk,” “Which department is currently responsible for the nuclear power plant? Can you get me that information?”

“The Federation’s Department of Engineering is responsible. The department has its own independent AI, primarily responsible for engineering surveying, modeling, and calculations. This isn’t within Eve’s or my domain, so it’s not under our jurisdiction,” Adam explained. “I can collect information through the Investigation Department’s surveillance network. If I find relevant data, I will report it to you.”

“Alright. Thank you, Adam,” Kui Xin said, reclining. “Regarding White Whale City…”

“You wish to resolve the matters here as quickly as possible?” Adam inquired.

“Yes,” Kui Xin confirmed. “Lingering here for too long is never good, especially with so many other issues waiting to be addressed. I’ve already set my sights on my next destination before even finishing things in White Whale City. I need to make haste.”

“I have a suggestion,” Adam offered. “You have two main objectives: first, to help Sea Without Light gain recognition, and second, to secure funds. Your first objective has undoubtedly been achieved. That leaves the second. The current situation is that you cannot afford to earn money slowly; you need to make a large score in one go.”

“And then?” Kui Xin prompted.

“My advice is to attempt direct communication with Polina and negotiate privately,” Adam suggested. “This way, the Popov family can mitigate their losses, and you can achieve your goals. Based on the mindset of those in power, they should be willing to accept your terms now.”

Kui Xin weighed the pros and cons. “I will consider your suggestion.”

A week ago, Kui Xin would not have let the Popov family off so easily. However, with Augus not being in White Whale City, Kui Xin would have to leave to pursue him. The Popov family, under Polina’s leadership, was a formidable opponent. If she refused to yield, Kui Xin might end up with nothing, having wasted her efforts. Forcing Polina’s hand could lead her to disown family members; she didn’t care about money as much as she cared about the family’s reputation. Adam was urging Kui Xin to make a strategic concession. For the sake of financial gain, a slight retreat was acceptable, especially since her primary objective had been met.

Kui Xin retrieved her communicator and opened her message page. A long string of new messages greeted her, most from Su Rong, with some from He Kangshi as well.

“Fer-de-lance, are you there?” Su Rong had sent this message over thirty times. She dared not use Kui Xin’s name or ask more, but persistently reminded her to reply.

He Kangshi’s message read: “Sis, Ash seems to have an urgent matter and asked if you’d contacted me. Please reply if you see this.”

Kui Xin sent Su Rong a reassuring message: “It’s fine, go to sleep. Rest early.”

Su Rong replied instantly with a single word: “Okay!!!”

He Kangshi then sent another message: “Four Leaves has been clamoring to see you all day. He finally settled down after falling asleep. [Image]”

The attached photo showed Four Leaves sleeping soundly, clutching a blanket, his face serene and as pure as an infant’s.

Kui Xin switched pages to message Amber, inquiring about the hostage’s safety.

Amber replied: “No issues, Abra wouldn’t dare to play any tricks.” He then sent another message: “How’s Four Leaves?”

“He’s doing well. You can see him often once things are less pressing,” Kui Xin promised, then forwarded the photo He Kangshi had sent to Amber.

Amber: “Thank you.”

Kui Xin put down her communicator and closed her eyes. On the game panel, visible only to her, were several lines of text:

“As the world changed, you calmly contemplated, bravely explored, and ultimately gained a portion of the world’s truth. You encountered danger and faced perplexing situations. Though the dangers were not entirely overcome and the mazes not fully navigated, you still found the path leading to the answer.”

“You have excellently completed the mission, and once again, you are the sole victor.”

“You have received the mission reward: [One Answer].”

“[One Answer]: You may pose one question, and based on this question, you will receive a ‘yes’ or ‘no’ answer. This opportunity can only be used once, so use it wisely.”

Kui Xin had already seen what the mission reward was during the three hours she waited for midnight in the Dark Realm. She calmly closed the game panel. It was clearly not yet time to use this reward. The truth remained unclear, and the opportunity to ask a question was too precious. It would be a waste to use it until the truth became more defined.

“Are you going to rest now?” Adam asked. “Goodnight.”

“Goodnight,” Kui Xin mumbled vaguely, and soon fell asleep.
