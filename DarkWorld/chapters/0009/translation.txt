Lancet was stunned.

Was he imagining things? When he'd said he was a Depriver with some real fighting ability, no one had cared much, but the moment he mentioned being the small-time boss of a startup, suddenly he was valued. Wasn't the Sea Without Light supposed to be the kind of organization where the boss led a horde of members through fire and blades, living on the edge of a knife?

"Which of my... uh, which of my talents is it that you value?" Lancet asked uncertainly.

The mysterious Sea Without Light interviewer in front of him answered, "Your business management skills, of course."

Kui Xin's words confirmed Lancet's guess, and he fell silent.

"Does the organization need me to start a business?" Lancet thought it over carefully before asking. "This is a player organization, and the first wave of beta players has only been here for a few dozen days. We have no foundation in this world, and the Sea Without Light only started recruiting publicly in the last two weeks. So you can't be asking me to manage something that already exists. You need me to create something."

Kui Xin nodded in agreement. "That's right. You catch on fast."

"I see. I understand now." Lancet nodded. "We're still at the stage of laying the foundations, right?"

"Exactly," Kui Xin said. "The Sea Without Light needs a legitimate company that can operate out in the open. That company has to provide the organization with a steady stream of funding, serve as a bridge between the organization and every level of Second World society, and also take on the job of screening talent."

It was clear that Lancet was a very cautious, steady person, and he also had some of a businessman's traits, such as a knack for seizing opportunities and a willingness to gamble.

"Then we'll need to prepare thoroughly to set up a company like that. There's a whole lot we have to get ready." Lancet thought for a moment. "First, a legitimate company needs a legal representative, and to be one, you need a proper, legal identity. I can change my face to look like all sorts of people, but I can't make myself legal identification. Second, we need to think carefully about which direction the company should go. If it's a tech company, what do we use as technical backing? Do we have technical talent? If it's a trading company, do we have suitable supply and sales channels, do we have connections... And the most important is the third point: we need startup capital."

"The third point isn't a problem," Kui Xin said. "The first point is a problem, but not a big one."

Lancet said, "Then let's focus on the second point... Do the higher-ups in the organization have any ideas?"

Kui Xin: ...Good question. I know absolutely nothing about business.

"Technical barriers can't be broken through overnight. Behind every successful research project is a mature research team, and one or two technical experts can't make up for that. Building a tech company isn't realistic at this stage, though it might be possible later." Kui Xin took the problem apart piece by piece. "As for connections... you know how players are getting by. Don't hold any unrealistic expectations on that front. Let's look at other fields instead, like advertising and marketing, or media operations..."

"Not a bad idea. There's a lot of room to work with there," Lancet mused. "My university major happens to be related, so I can finally put it to use. Actually, this world overlaps a lot with ours... Many people in both worlds are amusing themselves to death, swept along by public opinion, living in information cocoons, numbing themselves and indulging their emotions... It's just that the 'amusing ourselves to death' feeling is even stronger in the Second World. Purely from a business standpoint, the Second World is well suited to the media industry. As long as what we put out is eye-catching enough and keeps pulling in traffic, people will pay for it."

"Looks like you've got plenty of ideas," Kui Xin said. "Hurry up and write a business plan and hand it in."

Lancet's expression froze at that. For one brief second, he had a vision. In a daze, he thought he was back in the autumn recruitment season after graduating from university, with an interviewer saying to him kindly, "Why don't you write up a proposal? Think of it as a test of your abilities."

And so the naive Lancet of those days had earnestly submitted a proposal... which the company then took for free.

In the end, Lancet had angrily started his own business and successfully become a small-time boss.

Now times had changed, yet he never imagined that even after being promoted to boss, he would still have to write proposals, starting from scratch in another world and walking the entrepreneurial road all over again.

"How much startup capital do you expect to need?" Kui Xin asked.

"Registered capital for companies here starts at one million, and with early advertising plus staffing and venue costs, we'd need at least two million in startup capital... And there's no guarantee the early operations will be profitable..." Lancet said cautiously.

"Fine." Kui Xin's tone didn't change in the slightest.

By now Lancet had caught on too. The interviewer had agreed so readily, and the Sea Without Light didn't look short on money at all... He clearly remembered that not long ago the Sea Without Light had kidnapped members of the Popov family, given itself a massive round of publicity and set public opinion ablaze.

"Fer-de-lance." Lancet's eyes were full of hope. "Is our organization really rich? Loan interest in this world isn't cheap. If we can't scrape the money together we'd have to take out a loan, and we might be paying it off for eight hundred years..."

Kui Xin looked back at him calmly. "There's money. Enough for your startup. No loan needed."

Lancet's heart settled right back in place, and he was practically floating.

Back when he'd started his business from scratch, he'd gone through a mountain of guarantee procedures, running around everywhere before finally securing a few hundred thousand in startup capital. Things were far better now. Millions, just like that! With conditions this good, confidence flared up in Lancet's heart in an instant.

"I'll go write the proposal right now," Lancet said.

"Good," Kui Xin said. "From now on, you can team up with Wei Li and Golden Ring. Your abilities complement each other perfectly. When you have time, get to know them better, but don't pry too much, and don't reveal too much about yourself either."

Lancet said, "Understood."

A spatial vortex opened, and Lancet stepped through.

Now only Kui Xin remained in the meeting room.

She sat in her chair, thinking for a while. Wei Li's ability was mind reading, and reading ordinary people's thoughts was child's play for her, so Lancet could bring her along when negotiating deals. Lancet himself had no strong offensive ability, but Golden Ring's supernatural ability was fierce. He could make up for that weakness and act as a bodyguard.

A three-person squad that complemented each other perfectly.

Great, the recruitment drive had actually turned up some useful people after all... Kui Xin was very satisfied with what she'd gained from these interviews.

But Lancet alone wasn't enough when it came to people who could manage a company, and that kind of person was hard to find.

As for technical talent, Kui Xin's only candidate at the moment was Xi Liang, far away in Black Sea City. For technical reasons, the Sea Without Light couldn't open a tech company right now, but not being able to now didn't mean it never could.

Everything needed to be built up, and everything took time.

A media company was a good way forward. Kui Xin had suggested it because, like Lancet, she had seen through a certain trait of the Second World: it was amusing itself to death.

Control the media, control entertainment, control public opinion, control the channels people used to speak... and through that, control people's thoughts, manipulate their reason and influence what they said and did.

It was terrifying.

Kui Xin had already caught a glimpse of this trait in the uproar the Popov family kidnapping had caused online in the Second World. In fact, during that whole affair, Kui Xin herself had manipulated public opinion several times.

"Still not enough people..." Kui Xin pulled her thoughts together and began carefully recalling the information on players who had applied to join the Black Snake board in the First World. She had skimmed and verified most of their information, and she had paid special attention to and taken notes on a few who could be useful.

Managerial talent was far too scarce, but Kui Xin already had a candidate in mind: Xie Ganqing, one of the hacker trio.

He was the son of the richest man in Jingchu and ran several companies under his name, so he really did have plenty of management experience and a decent head for business. Everyone who joined the Black Snake board told Kui Xin where they were active in the Second World, and she knew Xie Ganqing was currently in an inland city, living a fairly stable life.

If the chance came up, she could poach Xie Ganqing.

She also needed to get the plan to recruit Xi Liang underway. The only trouble was that he was too far away, in Black Sea City...

Inspiration flashed through Kui Xin's mind, and she suddenly thought of a brilliant starting point for the business: Black Sea City.

She knew the city well, and more importantly, under pressure from the Investigation Department and the Special Intelligence Division, Mechanical Dawn had pulled its headquarters out of Black Sea City. They had moved away, so the city's greatest threat was gone!

The Special Intelligence Division only had a headquarters in the Federal Administrative Center, and its agents never stayed long in any one city.

That meant the only threat left in Black Sea City was the Investigation Department, and with Adam opening back doors there, she'd have plenty of room to maneuver.

Black Sea City also had a unique geographical advantage. It was a port city with convenient transportation and relatively close to the Federal Administrative Center, far better than remote White Whale City.

"Adam, what do you think of Black Sea City?" Kui Xin asked. "I want to make it the place where we start the business."

"Black Sea City?" Adam analyzed it for a few seconds. "Very reasonable. Very suitable. It meets your requirements in every respect. There are several plutocrat powers in Black Sea City that both cooperate and compete with each other, unlike White Whale City, where the Popov family rules everything single-handedly. All in all, it's a good place."

Kui Xin said with satisfaction, "Then Black Sea City it is, for now."

"Black Sea City really is a meaningful city," Adam said. "It's where you descended, where everything began, and where I met you."

"It is. I haven't been gone long, but looking back now, it feels like ages," Kui Xin said.

She got up from the chair and murmured, "I've got the twenty billion, and I've used Abra to stir up some trouble for the Popov family, the Resistance and the Special Intelligence Division. If all goes well, they'll be at each other's throats soon and too busy to bother with me... The startup team's been put together, and I've settled on where to lay the foundations... Mm, more than half of this whole mess is sorted."

The tangled threads were gradually coming loose. She'd had three urgent problems: collecting the ransom, screening the members and dealing with Depriver 777. Now only the last of the three was still unsolved.

"Have you decided when you're leaving for Floating Port City?" Adam asked.

"There are still a few small odds and ends. I need to read two people's blood: the interviewee who had a bomb planted in his heart, and Samanto," Kui Xin said. "And then there's one last thing I need to confirm..."

"Confirm what? Something to do with the Federation?" Adam asked.

"No..." Kui Xin paused, and a trace of cold killing intent crept into her voice. "I need to confirm whether Night Cicada is still in White Whale City..."
