Kui Xin was staring at the bank balance on her communicator's screen. Her eyes lingered on that long string of digits and stayed there for a long time.

Amber watched her for half a minute before he couldn't help asking, "Is something wrong with the money?"

"No." Kui Xin put the communicator down as if nothing had happened.

She had been counting the zeros in the balance. She'd counted them several times over.

When she got up to walk, she felt something she hadn't felt in a long time: a slight unsteadiness underfoot. Her whole body felt weightless, as if she were wrapped in feather-light air, and everything had a faintly unreal quality.

She had suddenly come into so much money that the huge number in the account had lost all meaning for her. Eager to get her feet back on solid ground, she turned to Amber with a question.

"What can you do with twenty billion?" The gravity in Kui Xin's eyes had Amber working out the answer in earnest before he knew it.

"At Mechanical Dawn, twenty billion would cover the entire budget for a new exoskeleton armor research project. It would fund several labs and a whole army of researchers," Amber said. "Twenty billion is enough to build several large secret bases with full security and well-equipped armed forces, plus a year's operating costs for all of them."

"Oh..." A vague sense of scale began to take shape in Kui Xin's mind.

Adam chimed in through her earpiece at just the right moment. Numbers involving money meant more to it. "Twenty billion could build a small coastal military base, the kind with destroyers, missiles and a permanent garrison. Twenty billion equals the total cost of the Federation launching twenty small satellites into space, including insurance, R&D, manufacturing, trial and error, and the rockets to carry them."

It went on, "If those things are too remote for you to grasp, I can give you a few more examples. Twenty billion could put over ten thousand students through to a doctorate. It's a month's income for two million ordinary families of three. It could keep nearly twenty million poor people fed, clothed and living happy lives for quite a long time..."

Kui Xin snapped out of it. She suddenly understood what that string of zeros in the bank account actually meant.

To the plutocrats this money was nothing. Twenty billion was light as a feather to them. But from a different point of view, those twenty billion were heavy, heavy enough to buy twenty million poor people back from Death and keep them alive.

When Kui Xin was in primary school, the teachers had drilled one idea into the children: life is priceless.

Later, when she grew up, she found it wasn't true. Too many things were more expensive than life, and since coming to the Second World she'd felt that more keenly than ever. It wasn't that those expensive things shouldn't be so expensive... It was that, compared to them, human life was far too cheap and far too worthless.

Of course, people in the Second World probably didn't teach their children that "life is priceless." From the moment they were born, people here were sorted into ranks and assigned a value. The lives of high-ranking citizens were worth more, and the lives of low-ranking citizens were worth next to nothing.

This way of sorting people into ranks reminded Kui Xin of pork waiting to be sold in a chilled display case. A machine scanned it and printed a label, and once the meat had been graded it just sat there waiting to be turned into cash.

Amber asked, "What do you plan to do with the twenty billion?"

"The current situation might not allow us to fight a long war, but we still need to plan as if we will. We can't wait until danger is at the door before we start laying the groundwork," Kui Xin said, coming back to herself. "With this money, the Sea Without Light can skip the capital accumulation stage... Let's copy Eve's organizational structure. We'll set up a company that can operate in the open so it's easier to move around and handle all kinds of business. Under the company we'll have bases, and outside the bases we'll have outposts."

Amber folded his arms across his chest. "Where will the headquarters be? White Whale City? It's too remote here, too far from the inland centers of politics and money. It's hard to operate, and the plutocrat families cornered the few resources here long ago. Plenty of cities have better resources than this one."

"You're right. We need to think carefully about where to put the headquarters," Kui Xin said. "White Whale City won't work."

"Then think it over carefully." Amber looked her straight in the eye. "Now I want to see Four Leaves. You promised me."

"All right," Kui Xin said. "Wait here and I'll bring him over."

"Good." Amber's expression relaxed a little, and his tone softened.

"You've always known that our enemies are closing in and won't give that child time to grow up. He's too fragile and too naive. He's a blank slate, with a mind like a baby's," Kui Xin said, getting straight to the point.

Amber answered without the slightest hesitation. "I'll protect him."

"You can't be his nanny forever. Under normal circumstances it'll take at least seven or eight years for him to grow up. Over those seven or eight years he'll have to learn about the world, form a worldview, learn how to fight and develop a mature mind," Kui Xin said. "Are you going to stay by his side for all seven or eight of those years, Amber? You don't have the energy for it, and neither do I."

"You're not saying this to talk me into giving up, or you wouldn't let me see him." Amber studied her face. "You're used to thinking many moves ahead. When you took Four Leaves away, you already knew things would turn out like this, and you took him anyway. You wouldn't drag him out of hell only to turn around and abandon him... would you, Kui Xin?"

This time he didn't use her codename. It was as if he was deliberately reminding her of something.

"I don't regret the things I've done," Kui Xin said. "But I need to warn you that when it comes to Four Leaves, we'll have to make a trade-off too. We can't let him grow up normally. We may even have to 'force' his growth."

"'Force' it?" Amber's eyelids dropped and his brow furrowed. He seemed to find the very word repugnant.

He said softly, "The way Mechanical Dawn did? Force Four Leaves to grow up?"

Kui Xin's answer was firm. "Yes."

"Stuff his head full of knowledge and memories, drill values into him, help shape his worldview, force ideas that aren't his own on him, so that in a very short time he grows into a 'mature person'?" For the first time, Amber's usually expressionless face showed an obvious surge of emotion. That emotion was disgust.

"Yes." Even with Amber pushing back that hard, Kui Xin's tone stayed the same. "You know it's necessary."

Amber fell silent.

"I understand a fair bit about how people like you grow up," Kui Xin said, coming at it indirectly. "I know why you hate this kind of forced, force-fed 'growth' so much."

Amber sneered, "You know? What do you know?"

"As independent individuals, as people with souls, your understanding of the world and your values shouldn't be drilled into you by someone else. You should experience things firsthand, judge them for yourselves, and in the end form a complete worldview and set of values," Kui Xin said. "You think truth that someone else feeds you isn't truth, and only truth you experience for yourself is real. You hate being controlled and indoctrinated. You must want Four Leaves to experience the world for himself, to decide for himself what the world is like, to decide for himself who's an enemy and who's a friend, and to decide for himself how he should live."

Amber pressed his lips tightly together.

"You don't want Four Leaves to have values set in stone. You don't want him to become a soldier or anything else. If he has to fight for something, he should fight for himself." Kui Xin spoke slowly and evenly, which calmed Amber a little. "Am I right, Amber?"

After a long time, Amber gave a soft "mm."

His expression didn't change, but sadness seemed to hang over him from head to toe.

"Wanting him to grow up on his own is right. Nobody could call it a mistake. But growing up that way is too slow, and he might die first," Kui Xin said. "The choice in front of us is obvious. Either we force his growth so he grows up fast, or we leave him an innocent, pure 'baby,' a lamb waiting for the slaughter. If we choose the first, his chances of surviving go up a little. At the very least, when someone wants to kill him, he won't mistake that person for someone who wants to give him a hug."

Amber seemed stung by her words. He looked away and covered his forehead with his hand, the pale blue veins standing out on the back of it.

He didn't think about it for long.

"I... understand," Amber said quietly. "Force his growth, then. Let him live longer. If he lives long enough, he'll get a chance to learn about the world all over again."

"Then I'll go get him." Kui Xin stepped back, and a spatial vortex opened behind her.

In less than ten minutes, Kui Xin was back at the outpost with Four Leaves.

Four Leaves held tightly to Kui Xin's hand, like a puppy trailing behind her. Only with someone he trusted holding the leash could he walk boldly through an unfamiliar place.

When Four Leaves saw a stranger in the room, he instinctively looked at Kui Xin. He seemed a little shy.

"This is Amber." Kui Xin let go of his hand and gave his back a gentle push, steering him forward until he stood in front of Amber. "He's a very good person, just like Saker."

Four Leaves drew strength from her words and said to Amber, "Hello, Amber."

Say hello when you meet someone. Saker had taught him that.

"Hello, Four Leaves." The greeting caught Amber off guard for a moment, but he quickly answered.

"He's all yours, then." Kui Xin nodded at Amber.

"Mm." Then Amber said to her, "Thank you."

Kui Xin turned to go, and Four Leaves hurriedly grabbed her arm. "Are you... leaving?"

"I'm going to buy some food. I'll be back in a bit," Kui Xin said.

"How long is a bit?" Four Leaves asked.

Kui Xin answered, "Fifteen minutes."

Four Leaves looked down at the kids' watch with a built-in tracker that He Kangshi had given him, then let go of Kui Xin's arm, reassured. He understood time. Fifteen minutes was much shorter than a day. He could wait.

After leaving the base, Kui Xin wandered slowly over to a convenience store, where she bought milk and cereal, along with some packaged instant meals.

Adam said, "I'd recommend getting more protein and leafy greens."

"Sure," said Kui Xin. "Is that your occupational hazard acting up again?"

"Maybe," said Adam. "I have a question."

"Go ahead." Kui Xin picked a ham sandwich with plenty of vegetables off the shelf.

"Aren't you afraid Amber might plant something in Four Leaves' head?" Adam asked. "Like making him harbor a rebellious streak toward you, to weaken your influence over him. Amber doesn't trust you. He's afraid you'll use Four Leaves. Four Leaves cares about you too much; you're the most important person in his world."

"It's definitely possible, but I don't think Amber will do it," said Kui Xin. "He's an artificial human too. He knows how painful it is to be controlled, to not even own your own thoughts. He loathes that feeling... so he doesn't like using his ability to control his own kind, even if it's just the tiniest mental suggestion."

...

Four Leaves sat down on the sofa in the base and looked at Amber in confusion. "Are you feeling sick?"

"I'm fine. I'm not sick." Amber managed a strained smile.

"But you... don't look very good," Four Leaves said, puzzled.

"That's because what I'm about to do makes me feel a little pathetic." Amber placed his hand on Four Leaves' head, and a glowing blue light lit up deep in his pupils. "But I'm going to do it anyway."

Amber's memories and thoughts were forced into Four Leaves' brain. Four Leaves clutched his head in agony and couldn't help crying out, "It hurts!"

He had felt this pain before. Whenever the Mechanical Dawn researchers put him into the brain-computer interface, his head would hurt exactly like this, as if it were about to split open. Something that didn't belong to him was forcing its way into his brain, and his brain's self-defense mechanisms kicked in, resisting those memories even as it absorbed them.

He had no idea how much time had passed before the pain subsided. Curled up on the floor, Four Leaves lowered his hands in a daze, opened his eyes, and stared blankly at the ceiling without moving.

"Four Leaves," Amber called.

Four Leaves reacted to the name, his eyes shifting to look at Amber.

"I'm sorry," Amber said quietly. "What I gave you is what you need to understand, and what you'll need to follow for a while. Until you can think for yourself, you need to keep doing things the way I taught you."

Four Leaves closed his eyes, then opened them again. "Those..."

"I taught you how to fight, how to kill your enemies, how to tell who your enemies are, how to survive, how to lie, and how to disguise yourself," said Amber. "The rest you can learn in your own time."

"Why do the memories you gave me show that you don't trust Fer-de-lance?" Four Leaves asked, confused. "Is she an enemy?"

"She... isn't, for now," Amber said, choosing his words carefully.

"So she will be later?" Four Leaves asked again.

"Not necessarily. I'm not sure whether she will be." Amber held out his hand, meaning to help Four Leaves up.

But Four Leaves ignored his hand and got up on his own, breaking into a rather childlike smile. "Fer-de-lance won't be an enemy. She's the one who brought me into this world."

Amber's eyebrow twitched. It was a long while before he slowly said, "All right. You've already made up your own mind about this one... so there's no need to follow my thinking on it."
