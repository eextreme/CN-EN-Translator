After a full day of resting in shifts, Kui Xin finally felt like herself again.

Amber's condition had improved a lot, and in another day his stitches could come out.

Amber still couldn't move around properly, so he stayed behind to recover in their hidden temporary safehouse while Kui Xin wandered the unfamiliar city alone, with a clear goal in mind.

This was a small city on the outskirts of Floating Port City called Mingshan City. It was nowhere near as prosperous as Floating Port City. Life here seemed to move at a slower pace, and there were more poor people and ordinary civilians.

Kui Xin stayed in contact with Amber through her communicator.

"I've been badly hurt before, but I never healed this fast," Amber said casually into the communicator as he changed his bandages and looked himself over. "Maybe raising my supernatural abilities to A-rank boosted my cell activity and my healing along with it. Or maybe your blood is just that potent. Even before you had supernatural abilities, your body was already incredibly strong. Your cell activity was on par with an Awakened's."

"Either way, the result is good," Kui Xin said. "Once we get the self-healing serum, you should be back to normal in about a day, right?"

"Pretty much," Amber said. "It'd go even faster if I could spend some time in a medical pod, but the situation doesn't allow for that."

"Too bad," Kui Xin agreed.

She came around a street corner and looked at the Investigation Department building not far off.

The Investigation Department building in Mingshan City was only a little over thirty stories tall, which couldn't compare to the ones in Black Sea City or White Whale City. Then again, a small city like this didn't need much manpower. A bigger staff would just waste resources.

Kui Xin was here for the medicine.

Only a handful of Federation agencies had access to rapid recovery serum, such as the Special Intelligence Division and the Investigation Department. Mechanical Dawn also produced it, but this was obviously not a good time to go rob Mechanical Dawn. The serum turned up on the black market now and then, but finding it was a matter of luck. Lifesaving drugs like that were worth more than gold, so if Kui Xin wanted some, her only choice was to hit the Investigation Department.

With Adam around, erasing the Investigation Department's drug records was a piece of cake.

Kui Xin waited patiently near the building until Adam told her, "You can move in now."

None of the security officers noticed when a service robot broke from its set routine, slipped silently into the medical supply warehouse, took three vials of serum that were about to expire, and then slipped past every security officer to the staff entrance on the ground floor.

Kui Xin used Shadow Walk to get close to the Investigation Department, met up with the robot Adam was controlling without any trouble at all, and collected the serum.

She didn't linger and was soon back at the temporary safehouse.

"Here, give yourself a shot." Kui Xin tossed the syringe to Amber carelessly.

Amber's eyebrows twitched. "You actually got it... You didn't cause a stir, did you?"

"No," Kui Xin said. "'Mirror' helped."

"Mirror... I'm really curious about this member." Amber took out the syringe and pushed the needle into the skin of his arm, and the serum slowly flowed into his vein.

He went on, "A super hacker, one who's a match for a super A.I. Mirror played a huge part in our escape, and now it's helped you get recovery serum out of the Investigation Department. That's no ordinary existence."

Amber chose his words carefully, and they were subtle. He only said "this member" and "no ordinary existence," and never clearly called Mirror "he" or "it."

"In the Sea Without Light, it's simply Mirror," Kui Xin said pointedly.

Amber smiled. "I see. Understood."

Adam's voice spoke privately in Kui Xin's ear. "I'd be surprised if he hadn't worked out who I am."

"True," Kui Xin replied on the private line. "I dropped hints back when I first recruited him, after all. Amber should have started guessing way back then. He just never said anything. As an artificial human who's awakened emotions, Amber is very good at thinking things through and keeping them to himself."

As the recovery serum took effect, Amber let out a breath, and his face relaxed a little.

His body temperature began to rise, and his wounds started to itch and tingle. That meant the serum was working.

A rescue mission couldn't afford to waste even a moment. Under the circumstances, Amber obviously couldn't take his time healing. Even two or three days of rest would be an enormous luxury. To get back into fighting shape quickly, they had no choice but to take drastic measures.

One of the three vials was used up, and the other two could be saved for emergencies.

The vials were close to expiring, and the active healing factors inside would lose their effect within about a month. Still, that was enough. Kui Xin's operation probably wouldn't drag on past a month.

"Time to move," Kui Xin said, glancing at the time.

Amber nodded, picked up the lighter of the temporary backpacks and walked into the spatial vortex Kui Xin had opened.

Staying in one city was too risky. From now on, they planned to change locations every few hours until it was time to strike again.

Sometimes they moved within the same city, and sometimes they went straight to another city to hide. They picked the locations and the distances quite randomly so that the Resistance couldn't follow their scent like hunting dogs.

Because they moved so often, Kui Xin and Amber could only rest in scattered bits and pieces.

In a gap between moves, Kui Xin seized the chance to read Depriver 777's memories.

The dark red liquid swayed gently in the glass vial. Kui Xin tipped her head back, and a single drop of blood fell into her mouth.

In an instant, an overwhelming wave of dizziness and nausea surged up. Kui Xin's vision blurred, and she swayed so badly she nearly fell to the floor.

Amber noticed something was wrong in time and hurried to steady her. "What's happening? That blood you just took..."

"I'm fine... Stay... quiet." Kui Xin squeezed her eyes shut and forced the words out through gritted teeth.

Chaotic fragments of memory swallowed her. Amber helped her over to the wall and watched her face anxiously. Her brow was deeply furrowed, and her eyes moved beneath her eyelids, as if she were trapped in a nightmare she couldn't wake from.

The moment she sank into Augus's memories, Kui Xin realized there was a problem: Augus's blood had been contaminated, and badly.

Luckily, she could still extract a few key memory fragments.

Kui Xin entered Augus's memories and saw everything around her through his eyes.

Darkness. Silence.

How silent was it? Augus couldn't hear a single sound or sense that anything existed at all. He couldn't even feel his own body or his heartbeat. It was as if he were floating in a vacuum, and every second dragged on like a year.

Immersed in the feelings buried deep in Augus's memories, Kui Xin could only sense one overpowering emotion: fear.

After who knew how long, whispers reached his ears.

Augus strained to make out the words but couldn't tell what they were saying... Before long, the whispering stopped... and singing began.

The singing sounded like hymns in a church, with many people gathered around him singing in unison. The melody was slow and low, and the singers' voices were hoarse and devout. But Augus didn't feel his soul being cleansed. Instead, his skin crawled. What they were singing didn't sound like a hymn to him... It sounded like a funeral dirge.

The song echoed over and over in his head. At first the voices were only a little grating. Then they burrowed deep into his skull, maddening, like a thousand people scraping their nails down a blackboard. After that the maddening noise grew louder and louder until his brain throbbed with pain. He opened his bloodshot eyes and couldn't help writhing and howling.

Only then did he realize he wasn't in any vacuum, and he wasn't dead either. His body was perfectly fine. He was just tied down with ropes to something like an altar.

A figure in black robes stood beside him, looking down at him with a silver dagger in hand.

He raised the dagger high in both hands and plunged it into Augus's chest, splitting his ribcage open.

The heart inside his chest was still pounding. Augus's eyes nearly popped out of his head, and he screamed.

"As you have promised, you shall be loyal to my Lord. As proof of your loyalty, you must offer up half of your heart," the black-robed figure said. He reached into Augus's chest and plucked out his heart the way someone would pick an apple from a tree.

The heart kept beating even outside his body. The black-robed figure swung the silver blade and cut it in two. He pushed one half back into Augus's chest, placed the other half on a black tray and carried it to the center of the altar.

He knelt and prayed, "The token of the covenant has been offered. Should he betray us, the Lord shall claim the other half of the traitor's heart, and the traitor's blood, flesh and soul shall all return to the Netherworld."

The hymn faded, and the prayer faded... Augus sank into darkness.

When he woke again, he was lying in bed. Augus bolted upright and felt his chest. It was smooth, without a single scar... If someone's heart had been cut out, how could they survive with only half of it left? Had it been a dream?

Augus looked around, dazed and completely lost. He was still in his own staff dormitory, and nothing around him had changed.

His communicator beeped.

Augus picked it up and saw a line of text on the screen.

"You must prove your loyalty."

Augus flung the communicator away as if it had grown a gaping, bloody mouth that might swallow him whole at any moment.

The instant he threw it, low laughter came from inside the room.

A blurry shadow stood in the darkness, and Augus hadn't even noticed it was there.

"You... Boss?" Augus recognized the man's silhouette.

"The boss" wore a black suit that blended into the shadows of the room.

"It's me," the man said. "That wasn't a dream."

"That wasn't a dream?!" Augus said, horrified.

"You promised earlier that you'd work for me. I invited you to join our cult, and you agreed to that too," the man said unhurriedly. "Once you've agreed to something, you have to follow through. What's wrong? Why do you look so angry, so frightened? Is it because when you made those promises, you never imagined you'd actually have to put yourself on the line? You were planning to betray me from the very start, weren't you?"

Augus took a step back.

His oath of loyalty had been fake, just a way to buy time.

Augus's identity in the Second World was a "bodyguard", the bodyguard of the man in front of him. On his very first day in this world, someone had noticed something off about him, and his "Boss" had taken a keen interest in where he really came from. So the man had kept him around and struck a deal with him: Augus would work for him, and he would pay Augus for it.

Augus had agreed, but he'd never imagined things would turn out like this.

"You were ready to betray me, so how could I not have a backup plan ready?" the Boss said with a smile. "Don't try to betray us, Augus. The secrets of this world and all its wondrous powers are far beyond anything you could control. You've already offered up half your heart... You can't afford the price of betrayal."

"Be a good boy and work for us," he said. "Do as I say. After you return, lure the players in, control them, and tell me who they are in the Second World. Either you betray your own people or you lose your life. Pick one."

"You said you'd pay me." Augus's mind raced as he tried to squeeze the most out of the deal.

"I will," the Boss said. "You want more supernatural abilities, and those are easy to come by. But you need to understand one thing. No matter how many abilities you get or how strong they are, you'll never be able to stand against us."

...

The world spun, and Kui Xin came out of the memory drenched in sweat.

She hadn't done anything strenuous, yet she was gasping for breath like someone waking from a nightmare.

In the memory, Augus had met the Boss he'd "pledged loyalty" to, and that Boss was tangled up with the Secret Cult in countless ways. The "Boss" in the memory had worn formal clothes and spoken casually, but he'd carried himself like a man used to being in charge.

Amber looked at Kui Xin with concern. "What's wrong?"

"I'm trying to remember someone's face," Kui Xin said, pressing a hand to her forehead.

The blurry figure from the memory took shape, and then so did his face. Kui Xin used Data Control to link up with Adam, encoded the figure from the memory and sent it into Adam's communication line.

"Do you recognize this man?" Kui Xin asked. "This is a very important lead. He's the one who gave Augus his orders."

Adam went eerily silent.

After a long time, Adam said, "This is... the Federation's Director of Government Affairs."

When Kui Xin heard that, she went just as eerily silent.

She and Adam had pushed open the door to the truth, and behind it lurked a terrifying behemoth.

Had Augus's Boss joined the cult after becoming Director of Government Affairs, or had he been a cult member all along, and that was how he'd climbed to the post in the first place? Just how many more bloodsuckers were there inside the Federation?

Mechanical Dawn, the Resistance, the Secret Cult... All these factions were parasites clinging to the Federation and sucking its blood, and the Federation was the real behemoth hiding behind them all... No... It was more accurate to say that the Federation itself was a behemoth made up of countless bloodsucking parasites! It was filthy and rotten to the core, and its insides were crawling with conspiracies, corruption and everything vile.

The Federation wasn't so much a government ruling the Second World as a fortress built out of everyone's ambitions and desires, a spiderweb spun from twisted things hooked into one another, an extension of every dark side there was.

"I've only just realized it now. It seems like we've only ever had one enemy..." Kui Xin said quietly.

Mechanical Dawn was a product of the Federation. The Special Intelligence Division was an agency under the Federation. The Resistance was a political party that had lost its struggle within the Federation. The plutocrats' interests were tied up with the Federation's. The Secret Cult lurked silently inside the Federation in disguise, and one of its own had even become a Director...

Kui Xin had fought these factions again and again, only to discover in the end that they had all been pointing in the same direction all along: the Federation!
