On her way back to the outpost with the food, Kui Xin was still wondering what Four Leaves would be like after Amber's mental implantation. But the moment she stepped inside, Four Leaves came over and nuzzled up to her just as he always did.

"I like milk and cereal," he said. "I like Fer-de-lance more."

Caught off guard, Kui Xin stopped short and stared at him. Then she smiled.

"Did Amber teach you that too?" she asked.

"He only transferred to me what 'liking' means." Four Leaves blinked. "Now I know what it means to like something."

Kui Xin glanced at Amber. He was leaning against the wall with his arms crossed, his brow slightly furrowed, but he didn't stop Four Leaves from showing her affection.

What did Four Leaves know about deep feelings? He only had a hazy grasp of them, and he wasn't much good at hiding anything either. Whenever he saw someone he was close to, he just wanted to cling to them, as if he were desperate to show every bit of his affection and trust.

He understood a great deal now, but when it came to the ways of the world and human relationships, he didn't seem to have grown at all.

A mature person didn't go around saying "I like you" all the time. Only children threw the word around like that. As people grew up, their feelings turned inward; they grew too shy to say "I like you" to any particular person, and they understood far better how much weight and meaning those words carried.

"Let's eat. Four Leaves, have you learned how to make cereal yet?" Kui Xin handed him the bag.

Four Leaves said, "In theory, yes, but I've never actually done it. First you need a bowl. You put the cereal in the bowl, then cut open the milk carton and mix them together."

"Go on, that's exactly it," Kui Xin said. "The bowls are in the storage cabinet by the wall."

Four Leaves clumsily got to work.

"Still just a kid," Kui Xin murmured to herself.

"I only made him understand what he needed to understand, and taught him what to do in which situations. It's like loading a preset program into his brain. When he runs into a problem, his body and mind will follow the route the program has laid out," Amber said. "Everything else, he still has to experience and learn slowly."

"Is that how you, Obsidian and Silver Mask grew up too?" Kui Xin asked curiously.

"Back then, mental implantation technology wasn't very mature. It was just a supplementary tool. We came out of the tanks early and grew up more or less naturally. Mechanical Dawn had teachers give us lessons. It's not like with Four Leaves, whose body and mind were both force-grown," Amber said.

Kui Xin was a little surprised. "They actually hired teachers for you..."

"Mm. After all, the technology was immature back then, and products of immature technology risk being scrapped." Amber gave a slight nod. "The teachers didn't interact with us much. They just drilled knowledge into us, and most of the time they taught online instead of in person. Intelligence was also one of the standards for whether an artificial human passed inspection. If you couldn't learn, you were eliminated."

Four Leaves stumblingly carried three bowls of cereal to the table, and he even plated the sandwiches separately, arranging them beautifully.

Amber said, puzzled, "I never taught him that..."

"Then it must have been Perigon Falcon who taught him." Kui Xin walked over to the table, pulled out a chair and sat down.

Four Leaves grabbed a sandwich and took a bite. "Wow, so this is what it tastes like! It's my first time eating one. I like this too!"

He hadn't yet had the chance to taste most of the world's food, so every flavor was new to him. When he first came out of the nutrient tank, even the softness of fabric against his skin had been a novelty.

Amber's gaze softened.

This world was cruel to artificial humans, but Four Leaves was bursting with life, taking in the world with the purest eyes. That eased some of the gloom in Amber's heart.

Kui Xin casually picked up a sandwich and took a bite as well.

Amber already knew what her face looked like, so it didn't matter whether she wore her mask or not. The moment she pulled it off, Four Leaves stopped chewing and stared at her face without blinking.

"What are you looking at?" Kui Xin asked.

"I want to... remember what you really look like," Four Leaves said. "Now I've remembered. I won't forget."

Amber hesitated for a moment, then picked up a sandwich and raised it to his mouth too. Kui Xin had bought food for three, and that included a share for him.

The three of them eating together at the table was, strangely enough, a little heartwarming.

Amber had never known what kind of atmosphere could be called "heartwarming." He only felt that it was quiet at the table, but not a cold kind of quiet. It was the kind of quiet that soothed you. He didn't dislike it, and it didn't make him uncomfortable.

Kui Xin had always thought Amber and Obsidian were the most self-aware artificial humans, but she didn't know that the two of them were still learning too, with only a half-baked understanding of many things.

They knew what family was, what kinship was, what sacrifice and empathy were, but they knew nothing about other feelings. Whenever Amber and Obsidian ate together, it was never peaceful. They wolfed down their food, rested on schedule, trained on schedule, and were always ready to throw themselves into the next mission.

"What an unfamiliar feeling," Amber thought, puzzled.

Soon his thoughts turned to the imprisoned Obsidian, and his heart sank in spite of himself.

"You're coming with me to do something this afternoon," Kui Xin said, setting down her spoon.

"All right," Amber said. "What is it?"

"We're getting new members, and we need to do the final round of screening," Kui Xin said. "Your ability is perfect for it."

Amber looked at her reflexively. "Aren't you afraid I'll..."

He shut his mouth, both puzzled and surprised.

Even Mechanical Dawn, which had raised him, had never fully trusted his ability. Mental abilities were simply too terrifying. Amber's supernatural ability needed no medium at all to activate. Kui Xin's Soul-Bewitching Voice needed sound to work, but he didn't. He could silently control a person and change the way they thought.

In all the time he'd been working for Kui Xin, he had never once met any other member of the Sea Without Light, and he knew why she did it that way. If he tampered with the minds of the Sea Without Light's members, Kui Xin would be left all alone, and that was a frightening prospect.

"I'm not afraid." Kui Xin picked up the iron mask beside her and put it back on. "I've made a choice too, a choice to place a certain amount of trust in you. I've proven my sincerity, and the way you've acted has shown some sincerity on your part as well. Now we can take things a step further and work together on a deeper level. Don't let me down, and don't betray me, Amber."

Four Leaves looked blankly at Kui Xin, then at Amber, trying hard to understand what the two of them meant.

"I'll do what we agreed. You let me stay to help you, so I'll do as you say," Amber said. "I don't want to be your enemy, and I believe you feel the same. One more friend is better than one more enemy."

"Mm." Kui Xin nodded. "Then we'll head out in a bit."

She told Four Leaves, "It's time to get your physical conditioning started. Set the treadmill on a low speed and walk on it for a while to get used to moving."

"Oh..." Four Leaves replied.

Amber said, "Come with me. I'll show you how to use the treadmill."

"Okay." Four Leaves finished the last mouthful of cereal and followed Amber over to the treadmill to learn how to operate it.

Kui Xin watched the two of them fiddle with the treadmill.

Adam said, "That scene just now felt familiar."

"Did it?" Kui Xin asked back through the data link.

"It seems like that's how it went when we first started working together on a deeper level," Adam said. "From that I've learned something: trust is gained through exchange. It can't be given one-sidedly, and it can't be demanded one-sidedly."

"That's right," Kui Xin said.

"Did you choose to trust Amber because you've seen through to his true nature and believe he's good at heart, or because he didn't do anything beyond what he was asked and didn't abuse his ability to influence Four Leaves?" Adam asked.

"Both." Kui Xin's gaze was calm. "But neither of those is the main reason."

"Neither is the main reason?" Adam said.

"I chose to use Amber because I really don't have any other high-ranking, mentally mature subordinates with the right abilities to put to work. Amber's right there, and it'd be a waste not to use him," Kui Xin analyzed coolly. "Even more, I'm using Amber because he cares about Four Leaves, and Four Leaves cares about me. How I treat Four Leaves will affect how Amber treats me. To put it heartlessly, Four Leaves is also a card in my hand."

"As expected. That explanation is much more in keeping with how you've always done things," Adam said.

Kui Xin stood up from the table and said, "It's about time, Amber."

Amber turned and walked over.

Four Leaves waved to him and Kui Xin. "Bye-bye."

A spatial vortex opened, and Kui Xin led Amber through it.

Amber was fully geared up, his face hidden behind a mask.

"What do you need me to do?" he asked.

"There may be spies and liars hidden among the people coming for interviews," Kui Xin said. "Your job is to control them."

"Control them, not kill them. You want to turn them around and get intel out of them?" Amber guessed her plan.

"Yes," Kui Xin said. "If we recruit more members in the future, our enemies will probably keep sending moles into our organization, one after another. Since we can't stop it, we might as well try to turn them against their masters."

"Understood," Amber said.

They arrived at an empty outpost. It had once been a billiards club, but the business had gone bankrupt and nothing had been moved out yet. A layer of dust covered the conference table, but that didn't matter. This was only a temporary meeting place.

There was a long conference table inside. Kui Xin casually pulled out a swivel chair and sat down, and Amber took the seat beside her.

She sent Su Rong a message through her communicator: "We can start."

Su Rong: "OK."

Two minutes later, Su Rong said, "Lancet has arrived at the designated location. Ready for transport."

A deep-blue spatial vortex instantly unfurled in the conference room, and Lancet stepped out of it, freezing for a moment at the sight of Kui Xin and Amber. The last interview had been conducted with no one present, the interviewer asking questions remotely; this time there were actual people in the room, which caught Lancet a little off guard.

"Sit," said Kui Xin.

Lancet sat down as told, saying nothing and asking nothing.

Su Rong: "Wei Li has arrived at the designated location."

Kui Xin opened another spatial vortex and brought the next person through.

She repeated this several times until every one of the shortlisted candidates had been transported into the conference room.

Lancet, Golden Ring, Wei Li…

"That's a lot of people," said Samanto, the last to be brought in, clearly startled. Samanto found a seat, gaze drifting helplessly toward Kui Xin, who sat at the very center.

A few minutes later, a pitch-black shadow abruptly rose up out of the floor.

Su Rong's shadow double took shape and sat down in the empty seat beside Kui Xin, directly across from Amber.

Amber discreetly sized up the shadow double, while Su Rong used it to study Amber in turn.

"Four in total. Everyone who's supposed to be here is here." Kui Xin swept her gaze around the room. "Let me introduce myself. My codename is Fer-de-lance, and I'm in charge of external operations for the Sea Without Light." She tapped the tabletop with her metal left hand. "You've all passed the first round of interviews, and I think highly of your abilities. But our organization has no use for people who might turn traitor at any moment. From here on, you'll need to prove you can be trusted."

None of the four spoke.

Kui Xin leaned back slightly in her chair and said softly, "Among the people in this room, at least one is a traitor-in-waiting… Feel free to guess who."

Wei Li's head came up at once, eyes sweeping over everyone in the room. Lancet's back quietly stiffened, and Samanto eyed the people nearby warily, mind already working.

Golden Ring's heart lurched. Holy crap—Fer-de-lance was making them play a round of Who's the Spy right out of the gate!
