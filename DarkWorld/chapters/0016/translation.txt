Kui Xin was worn out from overusing her abilities, but Amber had barely spent any energy at all.

They found an abandoned shack in the slums to rest in, while the rats and night birds under Amber's control scattered in every direction.

It was September 1st, 10:06 p.m.

In a little while, Kui Xin would have been back in the Second World for exactly two days.

"Who is this person you're looking for? What can he do?" Amber asked. "You came a thousand li to faraway Floating Port City just to kill him. He must be someone pretty important, right?"

Amber no longer spoke to Kui Xin in that cautious, probing tone. She held the upper hand completely, and her power was overwhelming. If she had wanted to kill him for his abilities, she could have done it at any moment, but she hadn't. That alone proved her promises were real. She really wasn't someone who killed for pleasure.

Very few people could bring out Kui Xin's killing intent. Even when she had decided to kill Night Cicada, she had been patient enough to wait a while for the right moment to strike. This time was clearly different, though. Kui Xin seemed... impatient.

She was desperate to kill this man. So desperate, in fact, that she had dropped the whole mess in White Whale City and rushed straight to Floating Port City.

"He has an S-class supernatural ability called 'Matter Reorganization.' It does exactly what the name says," Kui Xin said. "His name is Augus, and he's a Depriver. He has other abilities besides Matter Reorganization, but my Zero can erase them."

"Sounds like he really got on your bad side," Amber said. "Are you sure he's in Floating Port City right now?"

"Yeah. Very sure," Kui Xin said.

Amber thought it over. "You're two days late coming after him. He might have already run off."

"No... he probably won't run," Kui Xin said. "He knows I'm coming for him, so he won't run. I want to kill him, and he most likely wants to kill me just as much."

Amber looked at Kui Xin's profile, a little surprised. "An understanding between enemies?"

Kui Xin nodded. "An understanding between enemies."

Her enemies came in all kinds. Some were mainly out for profit, and some were lunatics who just wanted a bit of fun. But whichever kind they were, Kui Xin had that same understanding with all of them.

She found that a little sickening.

Kui Xin sorted out her thoughts ahead of time, working out what she had to do once she found Augus.

First, she had to find out how Augus had left the Dark Realm. Next, she had to get him to reveal which organization he belonged to in the Second World and who he was working for. Only once she had both answers could she safely kill him.

She couldn't be sure whether Augus's blood had been contaminated, or whether Blood Spirit could read it properly. If the reading failed and she missed key information, it would badly hurt her plans down the line.

It was a very long night.

A few hours later, Amber suddenly said, "I got a rat over to the containment dome inside the nuclear plant, and there's a big construction crew gathered at the edge of the dome..."

Kui Xin opened her eyes. "Get the rat closer and describe it to me in detail."

Another five minutes passed. The rat seemed to have made it close to where the crew was working, and the images came back.

Amber's fingers drummed restlessly on his arm. In a low voice, he said, "There's a huge hole in the dome... The crew is using robots to patch the breach, and every living person nearby is wearing a radiation suit."

Kui Xin froze. It felt like tiny ants were crawling over the backs of her hands, and goosebumps rose on her skin.

The containment dome had been breached and radiation had leaked out, which meant this entire area had already been exposed to nuclear radiation! Yet not a single word about the breach had gotten out. The crew was patching it quietly, and the people in the slums had no idea they were in danger.

If they lived here a while longer, maybe they would come down with all kinds of strange illnesses, and their children would be born with deformed hands and feet...

"Are there any Federation troops stationed there?" Kui Xin asked.

"No," Amber said. "Just the construction crew."

No troops meant the Federation still hadn't discovered the Dark Realm hidden inside the containment dome.

"Which zone's dome is damaged?" Kui Xin asked.

"Zone Six," Amber replied. "A containment dome doesn't break for no reason, and a hole that big... did someone smash it?"

"Maybe Augus broke it when he came out," Kui Xin said with a frown. "The question now is... how did he get in?"

How did he know there was a Dark Realm in there?

Was someone guiding Augus? Had someone helped him sneak into the nuclear plant? And did the people helping him belong to the same organization as whoever had ordered him to capture players?

"We can't stay here. We should leave as soon as possible." Amber got up and slung his backpack over his shoulder. "Too much radiation does serious damage to the human body. I'm an artificial human, and my genes were edited artificially. This place could mutate my genes and shorten my lifespan."

"All right, let's go now," Kui Xin said, picking up her gear bag too.

Since there had already been a nuclear leak here, Augus probably wouldn't stick around for long either. He might still be in Floating Port City, but not in the slums this close to the nuclear plant.

Out of nowhere, Amber asked, "What about the people who live here?"

"They'll have to stay here until the dome is repaired," Kui Xin said. "Do you want to tell them about the leak so they can get away?"

The people in the slums had long known they were living next door to a nuclear power plant. Anyone who could afford to move had moved away long ago. The ones still here stayed because they truly had nowhere else to go.

And how was the Federation supposed to resettle several hundred thousand people?

On top of that, this plant had actually started having problems years ago. Zones Three and Six had both leaked to some degree. The Federation had just kept it very well hidden, publicly claiming the domes were built to improve safety and never saying a word about any leak.

In recent years, the residents and the plant had lived side by side without incident, but Augus's actions had thrown everything out of control.

And it wasn't just the leak... To repair the dome, workers would have to go inside. They would discover the Dark Realm, the Federation might surround the place, and getting back in would become very difficult.

"We can't confirm how much damage there is inside the plant. From the moment that dome broke, the plant became one giant bomb. It could turn into a mushroom cloud at any moment and wipe this place off the map," Amber said. "It doesn't just threaten the slums, it threatens all of Floating Port City's tens of millions of people... No. Even before that, ever since the day the Floating Port City Nuclear Power Plant first leaked years ago, this place has been beyond control."

Kui Xin looked back at him. "The Federation has no way to resettle tens of millions of people, so they stall. They just keep stalling."

In truth, the Federation hadn't done nothing all these years. It just hadn't moved people. It had moved its economic center instead.

Over the years, Floating Port City's role as an economic center and transit hub had gradually been taken over by other cities. Even if the Floating Port City Nuclear Power Plant really did blow up someday, it wouldn't cripple the Federation's economy. The losses would stay within an acceptable range.

Amber looked like he wanted to say something but held back.

"Go on," Kui Xin said flatly. "You want to tell people the truth and save them, right?"

Amber gave a slight nod.

"Then go tell them," Kui Xin said. "I won't stop you."

"I can't just tell them. Nobody would believe me. I'd have to warn them some other way," Amber said. "Like making some huge news, the way you did in White Whale City. Use a big story to set off public opinion, and the people who believe it will believe it."

"That could definitely work," Kui Xin said.

"Could you... help me?" Amber asked quietly.

"Sure." Kui Xin's blunt answer stunned Amber.

His head snapped up. "Sure?"

"Because you've been useful to me," Kui Xin added. "You need to understand why I'm choosing to help you, Amber."

"I know... I understand," Amber said. "I'll always be on your side."

"Let's go. We're leaving." Kui Xin turned and walked out of the rickety shack.

Amber quietly watched her back for a moment, then followed her without a word.

He understood one thing clearly: Kui Xin had no reason to indulge his kindness. It wasn't in her interest and did nothing for her. That was exactly why her answer carried so much weight.

Kui Xin had no emotional ties to the Second World. She didn't like it here, and her deep disgust for this world was obvious even to an outsider like Amber. But Amber also knew that, although she seemed to have no lines she wouldn't cross, her standards were actually higher than those of Mechanical Dawn, the Resistance, or the Federation.

Maybe she wasn't as cold and ruthless as she made herself out to be? Amber thought.

"You agreed to help him, just like you agreed to help me back then," Adam said in Kui Xin's ear.

"They don't fit in with this world," Kui Xin said, out of nowhere.

"By 'they,' do you mean artificial humans like Four Leaves and Amber, or everyone who holds on to their kindness in this filthy world?" Adam asked.

"Everyone," Kui Xin said.

People like that were too pure and too hard to control. Because their moral standards were so high, they could easily part ways with their organization or their social class over a clash of values.

When everyone weighs gains and losses by profit, and suddenly someone shows up who doesn't care about profit but about human lives, the ones used to thinking in terms of profit look at that person like they're looking at a gorilla. They're so used to one fixed way of thinking that they can't make sense of anyone who thinks differently. The two simply aren't on the same wavelength.

Kui Xin understood Amber. She knew why he had this kind of compassion.

It came down to the fact that this world hadn't changed him into one of its own.

Take a family where the parents drink and beat each other. The child may pick it all up, copy what they see and grow into the same kind of scum, swallowed by their surroundings. Or the child may break free of that environment, come to loathe their family and treat their parents as a lesson in what not to be, growing into the exact opposite.

Someone like Amber had to be handled differently.

He had begged Kui Xin to let him tell the truth. If she refused, she would become, in his eyes, no different from the Federation, Mechanical Dawn or the Resistance: driven by profit, heartless, careless with human life.

Sooner or later Amber would drift away from her and become the next traitor.

Only by agreeing could she make sure he stood with her completely.

"Kui... Fer-de-lance." Amber caught up and fell into step beside her. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me." Kui Xin gave him a cold look. "Don't think I'm some noble person, and don't expect so much of me. I'm not who you think I am. You'll only end up disappointed."

"I know why you agreed," Amber said. "But I still have to thank you. You agreed, and then you actually did it."

He pressed a hand over his heart and spoke more earnestly than she had ever heard him. "I mean it. Thank you."

They left the slums behind and came to the city center, where tall buildings crowded together.

Kui Xin opened a vortex and stepped out onto the roof of a skyscraper.

The skyscraper was a luxury hotel. Laser lights were mounted on its top floor, projecting the golden words "Katerina Grand Hotel" onto the sky.

The moment the two of them stepped out of the vortex, Amber raised his gun and shot out every surveillance camera on the roof. His aim was flawless and he didn't waste a single bullet. Back in Mechanical Dawn, he had ranked among the best marksmen.

From the top of the skyscraper, they could see the whole city spread out below them.

Floating Port City was a dazzling jewel. Even at night it was loud and busy and blazing with light. The maglev rail cars hadn't stopped running. They wove between the towers, tirelessly ferrying travelers back and forth.

The people living in this city had no idea of the enormous danger lurking right beside them.

Kui Xin used Data Control to break into the laser lights' control system. The lights were purely decorative and served no practical purpose, so nobody had bothered to give the system any extra firewalls. She took control without any trouble.

The laser lights on the roof swung to new angles on their own, and the words "Katerina Grand Hotel" projected onto the clouds began to change.

The light turned a warning red.

The words in the sky now read: "The nuclear plant is leaking. The Federation is covering it up."

September 2nd, 3 a.m.

In the center of Floating Port City, that glittering city that never slept, everyone still out on the streets looked up and saw the line of words blazing across the pitch-black sky.
