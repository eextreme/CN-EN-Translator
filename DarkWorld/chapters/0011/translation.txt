A few minutes past two in the morning, Kui Xin had charged into the spatial vortex and arrived at Mechanical Dawn's Shaded Grove City branch. What stories lay hidden in Night Cicada's blood? She hoped it hadn't been contaminated.

According to the memories of Kui Xin's original body, Night Cicada had appeared beside her father, Kui Haidong, seemingly out of thin air, and from then on he had always run errands for him. She had once asked Kui Haidong out of curiosity where Night Cicada came from, but Kui Haidong had brushed her off with a few words and never given a straight answer.

Night Cicada might be hiding some sizable secrets, and not only Eve's.

Kui Xin gathered her thoughts, opened the vial, and tipped a drop of blood into her mouth.

Countless fragments of scenes came flooding in. The long-sealed door of memory swung open, each memory a yellowed letter. Kui Xin opened them one by one, reading the past life of "Night Cicada."

Of course, Night Cicada hadn't originally been called Night Cicada. He'd had a name of his own, a very ordinary one, but he had used it for such a short time that it left only the faintest trace in the sea of his memories.

That name had been given to him by the director of the orphanage. The director had named dozens of orphans all in the same style: very common and very rustic. Night Cicada always felt she named the children as carelessly as one might name stray cats and dogs, so he hated his name. Once he learned to read, he picked out two characters from his textbook that he thought sounded nice and put them together, and from then on he quietly changed his name to "Night Cicada."

The orphanage where he spent his childhood really was an orphanage, but it wasn't only an orphanage.

Only much, much later did he learn that the orphanage he had lived in was actually a Federation testing ground.

The children in the orphanage fell into two categories. The first were test-tube babies bred by the Federation. They let these babies develop in glass culture dishes, and once they were developed enough, moved them to the orphanage to grow up naturally. The second were homeless children who had one Awakened parent, a parent who had died in some accident.

Every month, psychologists and sociologists came to the orphanage to conduct research. They had the children fill out forms and play games, and they observed their behavior. Every week, a team of doctors came to give them all sorts of physical examinations.

Night Cicada had secretly peeked at a doctor's electronic notebook and seen the doctor marking ticks and crosses in it. At first he didn't understand what the ticks and crosses meant, but gradually he realized that the children marked with a cross would suddenly disappear one day and never be seen at the orphanage again.

Mother Director told them that those children had been adopted. They would grow up happily in wealthy families and live very different lives.

When the director said this, her expression was kind and warm, yet for some reason Night Cicada felt she was lying.

"Were they really adopted?" Night Cicada casually tried to confirm it with the other children in the orphanage. "If they were adopted, why haven't they ever come back to play with us?"

"Because they're upper-class people now, they're not like us anymore!" A little girl stacking building blocks gave a loud huff. "Their moms and dads keep their kids away from kids like us."

Night Cicada was about to argue when a boy beside him, someone he didn't know very well, suddenly grabbed his arm. There was no expression on the boy's pale face, and his voice was so soft it was nearly a whisper: "Don't ask anymore... Don't ask, and don't dig into it."

Having said that, he let go of Night Cicada's arm and looked away as if nothing had happened.

"Hey, you..." Night Cicada sized him up. "Are you new here?"

"Yeah, I'm new," the boy said.

Night Cicada tried to stand a little taller, but the boy in front of him was much taller than he was.

"How old are you?" Night Cicada asked, not very nicely.

"I'm eleven." The boy looked at him. "Looks like I'm a year older than you."

"What's your name?" Night Cicada asked.

The boy said, "You Wang."

Night Cicada had never come across that surname. "Which 'You' is it?" he asked.

The boy thought for a few seconds, then suddenly grinned. "You don't know it? It's the 'You' in youling, as in 'ghost.'"

"That 'You'? Then why are you saying it with a rising tone? Does it have more than one pronunciation?" Night Cicada asked, baffled. "Last time I had to copy that character out as punishment, and I clearly remember..."

Half believing him, Night Cicada went straight off to ask, and Mother Director's face darkened. "Last time you got this character wrong, I made you copy it out ten times. Clearly ten wasn't enough to make it stick. Go copy it twenty more times, with the pinyin, and bring it to me when you're done."

Night Cicada was dumbfounded. He walked out of the director's office in a fog and, as if in a dream, wrote out his twenty punishment copies of the character: the 'you' of youling, 'ghost.'
