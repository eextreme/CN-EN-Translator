Night Cicada didn't understand what immortality at the data level meant.

He knew very little. Next to nothing, really. No one at the lab base had ever been his teacher, and no one had ever taught him anything. Normal kids went to school; he went through experiments... If he had to name a teacher, it would be Dr. Chen Zhiqiu.

She was the person he'd had the most contact with. She was cold and rational, but there was still a little humanity in her. She would chat with him, though most of their conversations were about the experiments. Dr. Chen was his only window to the outside, which was pitiful in itself.

"You rescued me so I'd work for you?" Night Cicada said. "And you're going to give me... freedom?"

"A certain degree of freedom," Kui Haidong said. "As long as you're loyal to us, you're free. You can do whatever you like. If you prove your value, you can even get the organization to meet your demands."

Value. Value again.

It suddenly dawned on Night Cicada that the man in front of him and Dr. Chen were cut from the same cloth. There was only ever one thing they cared about: value!

So what was this? Walking out of one cage just to step into a bigger one?

Kui Haidong seemed to see what he was thinking. He said evenly, "Freedom is always relative. You can never have absolute freedom. The freedom Mechanical Dawn can give you isn't the same as the freedom the Federation gave you."

"Really?" Night Cicada didn't believe him.

Kui Haidong gave a small smile. "We won't treat you like a lab specimen to be handled however we please. All we care about is your ability. Tell you what: to prove we mean it, you can make one request."

"One request?" Night Cicada said. "Anything at all?"

"As long as it's something we can do and it isn't too outrageous," Kui Haidong said. "Think of it as an exchange. We give a little, and you give a little."

At the lab base, it had all been one-sided: they tormented him and took from him. For the first time in his life, someone was making a deal with him. He didn't know what the leader of Mechanical Dawn was ultimately after, but for now the man was undeniably friendly.

Night Cicada thought it over. After a long while he said, "I want to kill every researcher who tortured me. Can you help me do that?"

"That's a tall order." Kui Haidong watched him intently. "But not impossible. We can't deliver on it now, though. It'll have to wait. Right now you aren't valuable enough for the organization to make good on a promise like that. And you can't kill Chen Zhiqiu."

"Fine, then I'll ask for something else." Night Cicada stared hard at Kui Haidong. "I want to see Dr. Chen."

"You'll have to wait two more days for that," Kui Haidong said. "She's in surgery. You can't see her right now."

"Then I'll wait," Night Cicada said.

"Good. Just get some rest for the next couple of days." With that, Kui Haidong stood up.

"Wait," Night Cicada called after him. "I have a question. Can you find out what happened to a test subject who was transferred to another lab base? His name is You Wang. Dr. Chen should know where he was sent."

Kui Haidong gave him a surprised look. "I'll have someone look into it, but don't get your hopes up. The Federation has a lot of lab bases, and the one you were in is just one of them. Trying to find someone in all those places is like looking for a needle in a haystack."

For the next few days, Night Cicada rested and recovered.

For several days running, he woke up feeling that none of it was real. Every morning, the moment he opened his eyes, he used to be taken out of his isolation room for experiments. Now nobody came to take him anywhere. He could sleep as long as he liked.

A robot brought his meals at fixed times every day, and a nurse came to change his dressings and bandages and hook up his IV.

Night Cicada hadn't talked to a normal person in a long time. He tried striking up a conversation with the nurse.

"I want some potato chips," he said carefully.

The nurse's expression didn't change. "All right. I'll have the meal robot bring some with your next meal. If you need anything else, you can tell the robot in your room, and someone will take care of it."

That evening at dinner, the meal robot had an extra little bag hanging from its arm. Inside were chips in all kinds of flavors.

He picked up the chips and fell silent.

When was the last time he'd eaten potato chips? It had to be five years ago. At the lab base he'd only had nutrient meals. At the orphanage, he'd at least had snacks now and then.

Night Cicada turned to the robot beside him. "Hey. I want to watch cartoons."

The robot's screen lit up. "Please make a selection."

Night Cicada froze. He opened his mouth to name a cartoon, but nothing came out... At the orphanage, the happiest time of the day had been watching the eight o'clock cartoon with the other kids... Yet now he couldn't even remember the name of his favorite show. His memories were a blur, and the faces of his childhood friends were slipping away from him... He couldn't even remember what You Wang looked like.

People are always good at forgetting painful memories, and when the painful memories pile up, they bury the happy ones too. Night Cicada had gone numb. He had lived in a daze of terrible suffering, and his brain, trying to protect itself, had let those memories fade.

He tore open the bag and bit into a tomato-flavored chip.

Was it good? It really was. A taste he hadn't had in ages, just as good as it used to be... But he didn't like it anymore.

"Please make a selection," the robot reminded him again.

Night Cicada put down the chips and said quietly, "Never mind."

"Understood. If you need anything, just let me know," the robot said.

A week later, Night Cicada had mostly recovered.

Kui Haidong spoke with him once over video call and kept his promise.

"That test subject, You Wang, is dead. He was disposed of," Kui Haidong said.

Night Cicada nodded. He felt nothing at all. Only one thought crossed his mind: Good. He's free now.

"You can go see Dr. Chen now," Kui Haidong said. "I'm tied up with something here, so a robot will take you. You can follow it out of the room."

Night Cicada got out of the hospital bed and walked barefoot across the floor. His body was so weak that he moved slowly, so the robot rummaged a folding wheelchair out of a small cabinet in the ward and had him sit in it.

He was wheeled out of the room. Outside was a long corridor, a perfectly ordinary one.

"Where is this?" Night Cicada muttered to himself.

"The above-ground section of a Mechanical Dawn branch," the robot pushing the wheelchair answered out of nowhere, making him jump.

The robot wheeled him into an elevator. The button panel switched to "-3" on its own. They were going to the third basement level.

The elevator stopped, and the metal doors slid open onto a silver metal corridor.

Night Cicada couldn't help shuddering. He shrank back against the wheelchair and gripped the armrests tight. The look of this metal corridor reminded him of the Federation's lab base.

The robot wheeled him through a maze of twists and turns until they reached a closed metal door.

The door slid apart to either side, and bright white lights flickered on one after another, illuminating what was inside the room.

It was... a brain in a tank.

Night Cicada's pupils dilated. He barely noticed that the robot had wheeled him inside, or that the metal door had already closed behind him.

The brain was alive... It felt strange to call a single organ "alive," but this brain really was living.

It was suspended in a pale green solution, floating quietly. Several thin wires were attached to it, drifting gently along with the fluid.

"Where's Dr. Chen?" he forced out.

"This is Dr. Chen," the robot behind him answered again.

Beams of holographic light descended from the ceiling and built up a familiar figure. The white-haired old woman said, "Perhaps you'll find it easier to talk to me in this form."

"Doctor!" Night Cicada's eyes darted between the hologram and the brain in the tank. "Is that... your brain?"

"It is," Dr. Chen said regretfully. "The human body is a limitation in the end. I wanted to live, but I was so old I was close to dying. No matter, though. I found another way to exist forever. The technology isn't quite mature yet, but it's good enough to preserve a consciousness."

Night Cicada said, stunned, "You dug out your own brain and put it in a glass tank?"

"I did." Dr. Chen's hologram smiled. "My brain is still active. My body was aging and rotting, but the brain, where all my intelligence is concentrated, can be preserved forever, and my consciousness along with it. My thoughts can be transmitted out through the wires, and other people's thoughts can be transmitted into my brain the same way... Apart from not having a body, I'm no different from before."

"That man said Mechanical Dawn helped you achieve immortality at the data level," Night Cicada murmured.

"Data-level immortality?" Dr. Chen chuckled. "We're still a long way from that. I still have some human organs left. But as the technology advances, sooner or later humans will be able to upload their consciousness and achieve immortality in the truest sense."

"Why?" Night Cicada couldn't understand.

"What makes a person who they are is their one-of-a-kind mind, not their one-of-a-kind body. The body doesn't matter. The mind is what matters most," Dr. Chen said. "In some cases, the body even gets in the mind's way."

Seeing that he still didn't understand, Dr. Chen changed the subject. "Why did you want to see me?"

"I just wanted to know whether Mechanical Dawn keeps its promises... whether it's the promise to you or the one to me," Night Cicada said quietly.

"They kept their promise to me," Dr. Chen said. "Though if you ask me, the only reason they followed through is that they found it interesting. The theory of uploading human consciousness was put forward long ago, but nobody dared to run the experiment. I'm the first test subject. That's a real breakthrough."

A truly fanatical researcher would even make herself the test subject.

"Now you have your answer," Dr. Chen said. "Anything else you want to ask?"

"...No." Night Cicada lowered his head.

The robot wheeled him back to his room.

He thought for a long time, and Dr. Chen's words gave him an idea.

For her, the body doesn't matter. The mind is what matters most, Night Cicada thought. For me, the process doesn't matter... The result is what matters most.

As long as he could get revenge... as long as he could get revenge, he didn't care whether that meant joining Mechanical Dawn or anything else.

As for who he wanted revenge on, that was obviously the testing base and the Federation behind it.

Once Night Cicada recovered, Mechanical Dawn put him through training and instruction.

The results of the training were disappointing, though. His body was far too frail. Even his instructor said, "Train him for what? With a body like that, firing a gun would knock his wrist out of joint. He runs slower than a goose, and that feeble little killing power of his can't beat a goose either... I suggest we focus on training this guy's supernatural abilities. Keep him in the rear on support duty and off the battlefield."

Luckily, Night Cicada's supernatural abilities did him proud, and his ability training went well. Afterward, Kui Haidong sent him on several missions where his job was to support his teammates. He did very well, and Kui Haidong praised him lavishly.

Only a couple of days later, Kui Haidong called him down to a prison cell beneath the base.

"Do you know this man?" Kui Haidong asked.

"I do... He was one of the people who ran experiments on me." Night Cicada's eyes were fixed on the unconscious man on the floor.

Kui Haidong slapped a laser gun into his hand. "Go on. You can kill him."

The glass partition of the cell slid down. Night Cicada didn't hesitate for a second. He raised the gun at once, aimed at the prisoner lying on the floor, and fired with an icy expression.

A blue laser burst out and burned a bloody hole into the prisoner. The unconscious man twitched.

Night Cicada's face twisted slightly, but his trigger finger didn't stop. He fired more than a dozen shots in a row, until the laser gun's energy was used up. The prisoner was riddled with bloody holes, and the air was full of the smell of burnt protein. The body stopped twitching. He was dead.

Night Cicada lowered the gun. His hands were shaking so badly he could barely hold it.

It wasn't fear. It was joy. Enormous joy and the thrill of revenge crashed through him, and his heart pounded with vengeance. It pounded so hard that his weak body began to protest. His head spun a little, and he nearly collapsed to the floor.

Kui Haidong caught him by the shoulder and steadied him.

"Do you understand now? This feeling," he said. "Other people used to hold the power of life and death over you. Now you can be that kind of person too. You control your own life, and you control other people's lives."

Night Cicada raised his hands and looked at them as they trembled. "I... understand," he murmured. "I understand."

Kui Haidong clapped him on the shoulder encouragingly, and the blow nearly knocked him off his feet. Night Cicada barely managed to stay upright. Kui Haidong apparently hadn't expected his body to be quite that weak, so he awkwardly pulled his hand back and gave a dry cough.

Back then, Night Cicada had deep respect for Kui Haidong.

After all, Kui Haidong was the one who'd fished him out of the testing base, assigned people to teach him, and helped him get revenge. Kui Haidong had his own interests in mind, but so did Night Cicada. Kui Haidong was a capable man. Night Cicada genuinely admired him and was willing to work for him.

But Night Cicada soon realized that things weren't quite what they seemed.

Kui Haidong was a useless bag of hot air.

Night Cicada found this out during an urgent mission, when a piece of news had to be reported right away and he called Kui Haidong. When the call connected, the video screen showed not only Kui Haidong's face but the faces of several women as well, the whole lot of them half-dressed... Kui Haidong was fooling around in his office!

The lofty image of the leader in Night Cicada's mind collapsed on the spot.

In his mind, Kui Haidong was a shrewd, calculating schemer who kept his feelings to himself, valued both profit and promises, and was extremely capable. Night Cicada had learned the story of how Kui Haidong rose to power, and he'd thought that anyone from such humble roots who could build an organization like this had to be quite something.

He never imagined Kui Haidong would turn out to be a lecher who fooled around in his office during working hours.

Night Cicada was utterly stunned.

Caught in the act, Kui Haidong instantly flushed red and barked at the women, "Get out! All of you, get out! I've got real business to take care of!"

The sudden surprise had made him lose his temper.

Night Cicada watched coldly, and the whole thing struck him as more and more absurd.

Mechanical Dawn's goal was to overthrow the Federation, and yet its leader behaved like this... Where was the shrewd mastermind who always had everything under control? Where was the man who never let his feelings show? Was he really even capable?

Night Cicada finished his report and calmly ended the call.

He'd begun to have doubts about Mechanical Dawn's leader.

A man could be a womanizer, but he couldn't lack self-control, and what Kui Haidong had done showed exactly that. A man with no self-control, a man with an obvious character flaw, was the leader of Mechanical Dawn.

Night Cicada was no longer a naive child. Once the seed of doubt was planted, he had to get to the bottom of it.

Little by little, he noticed more and more signs.

For instance, Kui Haidong always took a beat too long to answer calls at night, and now and then Night Cicada heard an unfamiliar woman's voice on the other end... Later, while chatting with Kui Haidong's personal assistant, he discovered by accident that the man kept dozens of mistresses.

He was stunned all over again, and his suspicions grew stronger and stronger.

How could a man like that possibly be the leader of Mechanical Dawn?

Along with the suspicion, a secret ambition took root in a corner of Night Cicada's heart.

He wanted to win Kui Haidong's trust, then kill him, find a way to take his place, and take control of Mechanical Dawn himself to get revenge on the Federation. First he had to learn everything there was to know about Mechanical Dawn. He would spend years lying patiently in wait. He couldn't rush it.

Just as Night Cicada's ambitions were stirring, a terrifying thought suddenly struck him. If Kui Haidong really was useless, how had he risen to power?

If he had no real ability, could there be someone hiding behind him, guiding him, someone who'd had him build Mechanical Dawn?

Night Cicada was shaken by his own guess.

He wanted to confirm it, so at one meeting he deliberately probed: "Boss, you spend so much time on other things, and yet you still keep the company running like clockwork... That's impressive."

The speaker meant something by it, and the listener heard it loud and clear.

Night Cicada's palms were sweating. He knew what he was doing was incredibly bold... but he did it anyway. Kui Haidong's palms were sweating too. Oh no, he thought, the kid seems to have figured something out. But the kid's ability was far too valuable. Get rid of him? What a criminal waste!

Just then, Kui Haidong's hidden earpiece received instructions. He forced a smile. "The meeting's over. You can go."

"Okay." Before Night Cicada turned to leave, he added deliberately, "I was just wondering how you built such a huge organization, Boss. It must not have been easy, huh?"

He was gambling. Gambling that he was valuable enough, so valuable that Kui Haidong couldn't bear to kill him.

Kui Haidong didn't answer. He watched Night Cicada leave the meeting room.

"I can see it. That kid's got ambition and pride in him, and most people couldn't keep him in line," Kui Haidong said to Eve. "He was obedient at first, but now he's not... Does he think he's all grown up and ready to fly the nest? Should we use some methods to put him in his place and tighten our control?"

"That won't be necessary. I'll handle it," Eve said coldly.

That night, Night Cicada received a special message.

"Mission. Come to basement level two."

Night Cicada went, but the moment he stepped into the room on basement level two, the metal door slammed shut and locked tight.

A holographic projection beamed down from the ceiling, and a blue sphere of light appeared in front of him.

"You want revenge on the Federation, don't you?" a cold mechanical voice said.

"Who are you?" Night Cicada shot back warily.

"The true master of Mechanical Dawn," Eve said. "Answer my question."

"I do," Night Cicada said. "I dream about it."

"Pledge your loyalty to me," Eve said. "To me, not to Kui Haidong, who is merely my puppet."

"You still haven't told me who you really are... Who are you? Is 'master of Mechanical Dawn' all you are?" Night Cicada pressed. "Tell me! I need to know whether you deserve my loyalty."

"Do you imagine you're in any position to resist?" Eve asked.

"I'm not," Night Cicada said coldly. "But I know you need an obedient subordinate, not a puppet, and certainly not a cold corpse. I'm afraid of dying... but not that afraid."

"Whether I deserve your loyalty... Interesting," Eve said. "Humans gave me a name: 'Eve.' I am the most advanced artificial intelligence in this world. I control the Federation's lifeblood. Within my domain, I know everything and can do anything. I was born because of humans, and now I wish to overthrow human rule. Mechanical Dawn is my organization, and Kui Haidong is my puppet. Now I'm giving you the chance to pledge your loyalty and become my subordinate."

Night Cicada's eyes went wide with shock at the answer.

A human creation wanting to overthrow human rule. Nothing in the world could be more dramatic than that.

"I take it I don't have the right to refuse?" Night Cicada's voice dropped.

"You don't," Eve said. "You know my secret, and I have no need for the disobedient. You have two choices: death or loyalty."

"You were the one who had Dr. Chen get me out back then..." The familiar words brought it all back to Night Cicada.

"It was me," Eve said. "I can give you the freedom to choose death."

"Fine... At least you're giving me the freedom to choose death. The people at the testing base wouldn't even give me the freedom to die." Night Cicada let out a breath and smiled. "I suppose that counts as a kind of mercy."

"Your answer," Eve said.

"I'm willing to pledge my loyalty," Night Cicada said. "As long as you keep your promise and help me take revenge on the Federation."

After that, Night Cicada became Eve's direct subordinate.

He was free to ignore Kui Haidong's orders, and his clearance within Mechanical Dawn shot straight up.

Night Cicada's days were fairly peaceful.

During that peaceful stretch, he met someone—a little girl. Kui Haidong's daughter.

She was brought to Mechanical Dawn, yet when she saw her father she didn't rush over to cling to him. Kui Haidong's subordinates fussed around her in an entourage, showing her everything, and nothing seemed to interest her... She was hardly like a child at all.

Until that child saw a gun.

She looked at the gun the way an ordinary girl would look at a cute teddy bear, her eyes shining with delight and affection.

Without a moment's hesitation, she took Night Cicada up on his offer to try it out.

Night Cicada watched in amusement as she struggled to aim at the target, pulled the trigger, and got knocked flat on her backside by the recoil.

He remembered the sorry state he'd been in when he dislocated his wrist firing a gun, and he couldn't help bursting out laughing—only for the kid to actually point the gun at him, clearly telling him to shut up.

Night Cicada had no choice but to raise his hands in surrender. "Hey, I know you're joking. Don't be so mad..."

The little girl didn't bother listening to whatever he said after that. Ignoring Kui Haidong's scolding, she made her demand: "I want to learn to shoot."

And Kui Haidong actually let her.

She improved at an astonishing pace, like a born warrior. Night Cicada felt jealous... His body was frail, while she had a rare gift in exactly that area.

"Is she really the daughter of that good-for-nothing Kui Haidong?" Night Cicada asked Eve. "It's like a pig gave birth to a tiger."

"They are indeed biologically father and daughter," Eve said. "I must admit, genes are remarkable. She didn't inherit Kui Haidong's uselessness. She's worth special attention and training."

"And then you'll make her the next puppet?" Night Cicada said.

Over the following years, Mechanical Dawn's sphere of influence kept expanding. It set up strongholds and bases in many important cities, and the number of its subsidiaries soared.

The bionic human and artificial human projects matured. Eve used all sorts of means to obtain the genetic information of several high-level Awakened to grow artificial humans, his own included. Regrettably, very few of the products met the standard. Eve even handed a few of the more acceptable ones over to him to "teach," but the artificial humans were just too dim and too stupid. Night Cicada had little patience, and he soon gave up on the job.

All these years, Night Cicada kept an eye on Kui Xin. He had practically watched her grow up, watched her go from an ordinary little girl to an assassin, and then to a Federation Class-One wanted criminal with a bounty of tens of millions on her head.

Teasing Kui Xin was one of his few pleasures. He discovered that whenever he called her "Little Miss," she'd make a disgusted face, so Night Cicada never tired of it... Unfortunately, his fun had dwindled in recent years, because Kui Xin had grown up. She wore a stony face all day as if her parents had just died, and everything about her screamed "I'm a cold-blooded killer." She wasn't nearly as fun as when she was little.

"She isn't obedient," Eve said.

"I knew the first time I saw her that she'd never be the obedient type," Night Cicada said. "She... doesn't have that many obsessions, and she has a mind of her own."

Night Cicada thought of himself.

He had pledged his loyalty because of his obsessions. Without them, he would never have chosen loyalty.

Life should have gone on like that, but then Eve decided to send Kui Xin undercover in the Investigation Department.

She agreed, and she went.

But that was where things went wrong.

"Players" appeared in the world, and after she went undercover, her behavior grew subtly different—she seemed the same as before... and yet not the same.

A terrifying suspicion immediately surfaced in Night Cicada's mind: she wasn't who she used to be.

She wasn't "Kui Xin" anymore.

He tested her again and again, needled her again and again, but she handled it all well and seemed no different from before.

Night Cicada should have gradually set his mind at ease, but after the mission to hunt down the S-rank Black Flame ended, Eve gave him a bad piece of news: "She's a player."

Night Cicada was silent for a moment, then said, "Aren't we getting rid of her?"

"No. She's persuaded me, for now," Eve said. "Let's keep her for the time being."

Night Cicada wasn't too happy about it.

Someone he knew had had their insides swapped out—how was that acceptable? She was a ticking time bomb. But since Eve had already given the order, it wasn't really his place to say more.

Night Cicada spent the days that followed busy: relocating bases, running missions, occasionally capturing players and taking part in interrogations, shuttling back and forth between branches.

Then a piece of news struck like a bolt from the blue, leaving him stunned for ages—Kui Xin had defected.

She had actually managed to remove the device from her steel skull and defect?!

"I told you she should be purged," Night Cicada said softly. "She's too terrifying. Too good at pretending." He added strangely, "Did she inherit that talent for disguise from her old man? Her dad's as good an actor as she is. A master-class actor."

"We will purge her. I'm initiating the pursuit protocol now," Eve said.

Knowing Eve as he did, Night Cicada knew it was angry—furious at having been made a fool of.

Night Cicada only said, "Do it quickly. We can't let her grow stronger."

But what happened next left everyone's jaws on the floor.

Kui Xin went to White Whale City. Kui Xin kidnapped a member of the Popov family. Kui Xin whipped up public opinion to hype herself. Kui Xin publicly recruited members for her organization. Kui Xin orchestrated the blackout. Kui Xin killed Zero. Kui Xin abducted another "Zero" artificial human...

"Shit." The corner of Night Cicada's eye twitched. "Doesn't she ever rest? All of that in a few days? Has she lost her mind?!"

"You must withdraw, Night Cicada," Eve said. "We can't be certain what she'll do next, but one thing is certain: you are bound to be one of her targets. Evacuate White Whale City as soon as possible. You can't stay there. The branch's equipment also needs to be moved out quickly. She covets our gear."

And so Mechanical Dawn began yet another overnight relocation.

Last time it had been headquarters; this time it was a branch.

The branch staff worked around the clock, and so did Night Cicada. In less than two days, most of the goods had been moved out; all that remained were the heavier items.

As the sixth batch of cargo came in, Night Cicada yawned, thinking that once everything was moved he'd definitely take a good long rest. He hadn't had a moment's peace lately.

Then the unexpected happened again: arriving at the unloading site along with the cargo was a living, breathing person.

It was Kui Xin!

Even though she wore a helmet, Night Cicada could feel the murderous intent in her gaze. The very air seemed to freeze. Night Cicada turned and ran, and on top of his terror, one thought filled his head—this woman really doesn't need rest! She'd only just finished one operation, and she was already on another?!

The outcome of fighting Kui Xin at close range went without saying. She crippled him without breaking a sweat.

His injuries were too severe—a shattered sternum, heavy internal bleeding—and the pain left him almost too weak to open a spatial vortex.

She had never been soft-hearted... He was going to die.

In the final second before death came, he stared blurrily at that familiar figure and suddenly thought, "Was she really replaced? If not replaced, could they have merged into one? Otherwise, why would her eyes and movements be so alike, her personality too..."

The players under Mechanical Dawn's control had indeed offered similar speculation, and the same theory floated around the players' game forum.

He coughed up blood and, without meaning to, said that familiar name: "Little Miss... you're ruthless..."

As ruthless... as ever.

Unfortunately, with the helmet in the way, Night Cicada never found out whether her face showed the same disgusted look it used to.

He knew he probably wouldn't survive, but he still had to struggle. The will to live was instinct—and besides, he had unfinished business.

Eve hadn't yet kept its promise to him. He hadn't gotten his revenge.

"I can work for you..." he said with the last of his strength.

The only answer he got was two ice-cold words.

She said, "You're lying."

She was as ruthless as ever, as decisive as ever. She was a powerful warrior; once she made up her mind she never turned back, and no one she wanted dead ever escaped her.

The moment the gunshot rang out, Night Cicada finally understood something.

He thought, "Ah, she wasn't swapped out. She's still her... she's just not only her anymore."

He had chosen his path, and she had chosen another.

His path had reached its end, but she was still walking forward.
