“Your Anchor Theory may not be entirely correct,” Li Wanran said in a low voice. “But it is certainly a possibility. Have you told anyone else about this?”

“No,” Kui Xin replied.

“That’s good. Thank you for telling me specifically. This speculation is too astonishing… Do you plan to make it public on the forum?” Li Wanran asked.

“We have that intention, but we can wait a few days to observe the situation,” Kui Xin said. “Players must understand their predicament, their position, and the role they play in the world’s fusion. With understanding, they won’t be caught unprepared when disaster strikes and make wrong decisions.”

Li Wanran looked at Kui Xin with a worried and heavy expression, delicately softening her tone. “I understand what you mean. World fusion will inevitably lead to severe consequences. Players certainly need to be prepared. Before becoming players, most people were just ordinary individuals. The Anchor Theory… it’s not suitable for large-scale discussion in the short term. Firstly, we have no way to confirm its accuracy, and secondly, we fear players might act irrationally…”

“We will disclose it cautiously,” Kui Xin assured.

Li Wanran continued, “In the Second World, although players are moving towards unity, the Federation is pressuring them too tightly. Players lack channels for information transmission and means of mutual contact. Sea Without Light made a big splash in White Whale City, and now any capable player will undoubtedly try their best to reach White Whale City and converge with Sea Without Light. All players share the same stance. What are Black Snake’s plans for players who cannot make it to White Whale City?”

“We already have preliminary plans that need to be implemented once we return to the Second World,” Kui Xin explained. “Sea Without Light is a player organization, but it’s not the only one. All player organizations can maintain contact and assist each other. Professor Li, if you encounter any difficulties in the Second World, Sea Without Light will do its best to provide help.”

“Thank you. I will do the same on my end,” Li Wanran said, glancing at her watch. “I need to continue guarding the tobacco factory. Goodbye.”

“Goodbye,” Kui Xin replied.

Li Wanran turned and left, Kui Xin remaining in place.

Once Li Wanran’s figure completely disappeared, Kui Xin looked towards the darkness to her front right, where weeds and bushes grew wildly, perfect for hiding.

The second person to meet Kui Xin appeared: Tang Guan.

His appearance was no different from a few days ago; his mental state was stable, his gaze calm. It seemed he had spent the past week back in the Second World in relative peace, at least without life-threatening danger.

“I overheard your conversation,” Tang Guan stated directly.

“Mm, it doesn’t matter,” Kui Xin asked him the same question. “Did you encounter any anomalies during your world-hopping?”

“Are you asking about temporal anomalies, or…” Tang Guan’s eyes, visible outside his mask, flashed with a peculiar emotion. He met Kui Xin’s gaze, hidden within her hood, with a probing look.

“I’m asking about that ‘or’,” Kui Xin confirmed something from Tang Guan’s unfinished sentence.

“The ruby and the sapphire, which do you choose?” Tang Guan’s voice dropped to an almost imperceptible level. If Kui Xin hadn’t possessed astonishing hearing, she would have missed his words entirely.

Kui Xin’s heart skipped a beat. She and Tang Guan gazed at each other in silence.

His voice was a little hoarse. “The Anchor Theory, Kui Xin said that special players might be the anchors. How can one identify if they are an anchor? Others haven’t encountered the ruby and sapphire question, but we did. We are special. Could we be… anchors?”

Beneath Tang Guan’s calm exterior lay immense confusion and fear. He desperately wanted to protect his family, to protect his home. But what if he himself was one of the factors causing his family and home to be threatened? He was engulfed by a sense of helplessness, his mind in disarray. The Human-Faced Ghost cackled in his consciousness, and the anomalous creature trapped within his body stirred, wanting to break free from its cage.

“White Dove,” Kui Xin took a step forward, approaching Tang Guan.

Tang Guan suddenly came to his senses, struggling to control himself. The Human-Faced Ghost’s laughter subsided, and the clamor of the anomalous creature faded. He took a step back, his heart pounding violently, his chest feeling tight and suffocating.

“Sorry, I’m not in a good state. I lost focus,” Tang Guan said.

“You look like more than just ‘not in a good state’,” Kui Xin observed his sweaty forehead. “White Dove, you gave yourself that codename. You probably desire a peaceful life, right?”

“Yes,” Tang Guan forced out a smile. “Perhaps the wish for peace cannot be fulfilled. That choice question, is it asking us to make a decision about our destiny?”

“I’m not sure,” Kui Xin replied.

Tang Guan continued, “Have you ever thought about why it’s you? And why it’s me?”

“I’ve thought about it, but I couldn’t guess the answer. It’s an answer that cannot be probed. All that lies before us are choices,” Kui Xin said. “Having choices is good; it represents a hint, a chance for a favorable turn in the future. Without choices and hints, if I suddenly face a major decision in the future, I might not be able to make the optimal judgment.”

“The scales of choice mean a hint to you?” Tang Guan mulled over Kui Xin’s words.

“Let me put it this way. You seem to understand the situation as if you have cancer and the doctor is giving you a choice between conservative treatment or giving up. In reality, it’s more like you might have cancer. You met a doctor on the road, and the doctor is reminding you to get checked and to be mentally prepared for the possibility of cancer,” Kui Xin explained. “It’s different.”

“Your analogy is strange, a mix of pessimism and optimism,” Tang Guan said with a bitter smile. “But you’re right, it is different. Receiving a hint might be a good thing… maybe we don’t have ‘cancer’.”

He could only comfort himself this way.

“Is there something you need from me?” Kui Xin asked.

Tang Guan adjusted his emotions, and once he felt stable, he said, “I want to ask you a question, do you know ‘Tang Guan’?”

Kui Xin was surprised by Tang Guan’s direct revelation of his identity, but after a moment’s thought, she understood. Tang Guan was likely still trapped in the Second World, being experimented on by Mechanical Dawn. If he wanted to escape, he would inevitably need external help. He had overheard Kui Xin and Li Wanran’s conversation and knew Kui Xin belonged to “Sea Without Light,” a player organization. During his brief return, he had browsed the forums and seen posts. Sea Without Light’s sudden emergence had been like a thunderclap, causing a huge stir on the forums-it was arguably the strongest and most renowned player organization.

“I know him,” Kui Xin said softly. “You are Tang Guan, I know.”

Tang Guan pulled a knife from his pocket and slashed his left arm. Blood flowed from the wound, which did not heal. He switched to his other hand and cut his right arm. This time, blue blood flowed out-the blood of a variant species! The wound healed quickly. Tang Guan wiped away the blood, leaving no trace on his skin.

“I am a monster hybrid. The monster returned to this world with me. My body is assimilating with my Second World body. Do you know what that means?” Tang Guan said in a low voice. “As soon as I heard you talk about the Anchor Theory, I realized it might be true. The mutation started with players, with me. I am one of the focal points of the fusion between the two worlds.

I don’t know what to do anymore, Fer-de-lance,” Tang Guan said painfully. “What I originally thought was to find a way to convince you and cooperate to escape Mechanical Dawn once I confirmed you meant no harm… But given the current situation, would my death in the Second World be more beneficial to the First World?”

“No, Fang Zhi, the Blood-Variant, also died, but his death still caused those mutations. When nothing is certain, your self-sacrifice might be meaningless. It could even accelerate the Anchor’s fixation on a certain place, like the tobacco factory,” Kui Xin said, watching his pained expression. She softened her tone slightly. “You don’t need to sacrifice yourself now. Keep your life.”

Tang Guan pursed his lips and looked at Kui Xin.

“Where were you located in the Second World?” Kui Xin asked.

“Floating Port City,” Tang Guan replied.

“One hundred percent sure?”

“Pretty much,” Tang Guan said. “When I lost consciousness, the monster inhabiting me kept an eye on the outside. It overheard the researchers surrounding me saying so.”

“Are you in danger?” Kui Xin asked.

Tang Guan said, “As long as I still have research value, I won’t die.”

“Are there any other people imprisoned with you?”

Tang Guan shook his head. “I’m a high-risk experimental subject, so I’m held in solitary confinement. I only see researchers normally.”

Kui Xin nodded. “I understand. Sea Without Light will rescue you when the time is right.”

Tang Guan couldn’t help but ask, “You just agreed to it like that?”

“What else? If we can save a fellow being, we will, and we always have. If we want to gain anything from rescuing people, it’s the hope that players will become more unified, forming a force that no one dares to easily attack. This is beneficial to us, and beneficial to all players. Those who are isolated and helpless are destined not to survive long.”

Tang Guan’s eyebrows relaxed slightly. “You’re right.”

“I’m sorry about what happened on the ship… I couldn’t save you then,” Kui Xin said.

“It’s not your fault,” Tang Guan smiled faintly. “We both have our difficulties.”

Kui Xin confirmed, “Can you control the monster now?”

“In this world, yes,” Tang Guan said. “We’ve reached a truce. In the Second World, it won’t randomly cause trouble or control me anymore because it also wants to escape the cage of Mechanical Dawn.”

“Do you have a way to separate yourself from the monster?” Kui Xin asked.

“It’s difficult,” Tang Guan said. “But I can try to suppress it.” As long as his symbiotic supernatural ability continued to level up, he might be able to counter the Human-Faced Ghost again. Back on the Kraken, he had also suppressed the Human-Faced Ghost for a long time until his mental breakdown led to the Human-Faced Ghost completely taking control of him.

Kui Xin asked in detail, “To what extent has your symbiosis with the monster reached?”

“Very deep. It can even know my thoughts,” Tang Guan said with a complex expression.

“Then it’s troublesome,” Kui Xin said. “You need to… be prepared.”

“Prepared to fail and give up my life?” Tang Guan said. “I’ve long been prepared. In my situation, the probability of dying is greater than the probability of surviving.”

“Good,” Kui Xin said. “Then try to hold on in that world for a while longer, White Dove.”

As they parted, Kui Xin got Tang Guan’s contact information so they could communicate promptly if any special situations arose in the First World.

Today was… August 24th.

Tomorrow, August 25th, was the university registration day, which would last for two days.

Kui Xin tapped her phone and scrolled through her messages. She saw a message from her high school homeroom teacher sent around 21:00 on the 23rd.

“Little Xin, would you like to come to my place for lunch tomorrow? Have you prepared everything for university? Since it’s your first time going to university, I’m worried you might not know what to bring, so I’ve prepared a few things for you. See if you need them, and if so, take them to school.”

The teacher had always been protective of Kui Xin’s pride. Knowing her family wasn’t well-off, the teacher had helped her both openly and subtly, but without making her feel like she was receiving charity that would wound her self-esteem.

Kui Xin no longer lacked money. She had earned some living expenses by tutoring Su Rong during this period. Xie Ganqing had also transferred 100,000 yuan to her as compensation previously. Although she had spent some, it was more than enough to cover her university expenses.

Kui Xin looked at the message on her phone and felt a sense of temporal dislocation. In the teacher’s timeline, she had only eaten lunch with Kui Xin a few weeks ago and met her once. But in Kui Xin’s own timeline, it had been a very, very long time since she had last eaten at her teacher’s house.

She had been busy every moment, with no time to think about her own life, no energy to consider what to do when she went to university. Her original plans were perfectly laid out: work part-time during the summer to earn some money, study hard in university, join an interesting club in her spare time to make friends, participate in student council activities to train herself, build a good relationship with her teacher, and then proceed with her studies, graduate, work to earn money, and pursue a master’s degree.

Now, all these plans were in disarray. Kui Xin wasn’t considering earning money or her daily life, but rather how to destroy her enemies and protect herself in the game between two worlds. Even if Kui Xin could eventually return to a peaceful life, her mindset would never go back. The Second World hadn’t brought Kui Xin physical pain, but a profound change on a spiritual level.

Scrolling further down, she saw a message from Su Rong asking how tonight’s situation was and if she needed her to deploy shadows for patrol. Kui Xin replied: “No need, rest. You’re starting school too, Rong Rong. Get some good rest.”

Su Rong: “Sister Xin Xin, save me! You saying that has triggered my pre-school anxiety!!”

Kui Xin couldn’t help but chuckle and looked at the next message.

He Kangshi: “Boss, Augus sent another message, asking why I’m not continuing to kill players anymore, if I don’t want to continue the bet. He also asked if I wanted to choose between a ruby or a sapphire… What does he mean? Oh, and the forum announcement shows that Depriver 777 killed another player this week…”

The smile that had lingered on Kui Xin’s face vanished instantly. Augus’s message tonight was as nauseating as stepping on a pile of fresh feces while walking down the street. She typed expressionlessly: “Tell that idiot to crawl away.”

He Kangshi: “Huh?”

He Kangshi said cautiously, “Boss, are you in a bad mood tonight?”

Kui Xin was exhausted physically and mentally, too lazy to play mind games with Augus. “Forget it. Tell him if he wants to kill me, he should come to Tongling City quickly. If not, then get lost. His incessant chattering is annoying.”

Kui Xin’s words went through several twists and turns in He Kangshi’s mind. He was shocked, then excited, and then filled with worry.

He opened the translation software and cleverly translated the message in accordance with Black Snake’s usual enigmatic speaking style: “I’m starting to get bored with this tedious game. Let’s play something more exciting. I’m in Tongling City. Come and kill me if you dare. Don’t just talk the talk, or I’ll start doubting your true strength.”

Excellent, as high-brow as ever, and he even threw in a bit of a challenge.

Within moments, He Kangshi received a reply.

The response was a single, concise sentence: “Since you’ve so warmly invited me, of course I must accept.”

Upon returning home, Kui Xin immediately went to check on the small white mouse cage she kept in the bathroom.

Mouse number three, the one she was focusing on, had already devoured all the remaining food in the cage. Its body had undergone a noticeable transformation. First, its spinal column bulged significantly, followed by the shedding of its fur. Its long tail had even begun to develop faint barbs.

“It really mutated. The contaminated blood can indeed trigger mutations,” Kui Xin confirmed her hypothesis.

The remaining few white mice, injected with highly diluted variant species blood, showed no signs of mutation and were still lively and active.

Kui Xin made a preliminary judgment: “After dilution, the variant species’s blood indeed loses its contaminating properties…”

She breathed a sigh of relief, took a shower, ate, and finally lay down on her bed half an hour later.
