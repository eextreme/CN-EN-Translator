Without hesitating, Kui Xin took Amber to another city three hundred kilometers away.

"Where's the nearest clinic?" The moment she asked, her eyepiece filled with tiny green dots marking every hospital and private clinic in the city.

Kui Xin picked out the closest one, a private clinic two kilometers away, and opened a spatial vortex to the coordinates Adam gave her.

The vortex opened right in the clinic's front lobby. Luckily, it was the middle of the night and the clinic was empty apart from the nurse and doctor on duty.

The nurse at the reception desk was dozing and didn't notice that two people had just appeared in the clinic.

Kui Xin shook her awake. "Wake up! I need you to save someone!"

The nurse's head jerked up. When she took in Kui Xin and Amber's gear and the bloody wounds, she stood and stepped back, terrified. "We don't treat people we can't identify! Leave right now or I'll press the alarm!"

The doctor heard the commotion and pushed open the door of the treatment room to look out. Fear crossed his face as well.

"Shut up!" Kui Xin activated her Soul-Bewitching Voice and spoke coldly. "Doctor, get the medicine and surgical instruments ready for emergency surgery. Nurse, lock the clinic doors, turn off the outside lights and put up the closed sign. Then clean up every drop of blood on the floor. Don't leave any genetic material behind."

The nurse gave a dazed nod and went to lock the doors as she'd been told.

The doctor said blankly, "Please come to the examination room first..."

Every step Kui Xin took left a dark red smear on the floor. If the bleeding didn't stop soon, Amber would bleed out. His wounds covered too much of his body.

The doctor did a quick examination and gave Amber an emergency hemostatic shot, but his temperature kept falling. He had already gone into shock. His face was white as paper and had the look of death about it.

"He needs a transfusion," the doctor said. "This is a small clinic. Our blood bank is understocked and we haven't restocked yet, so I can't give him one."

"Use mine," Kui Xin said.

"Once the blood is drawn, it has to be tested and filtered through special equipment. Your blood type might not match the patient's," the doctor said.

"I know it matches. Hurry up," Kui Xin said, her face dark.

The needle went into Kui Xin's arm. Dark red liquid ran through the clear tubing into the machine, which quickly finished filtering it, and then the blood flowed out through another tube and into Amber's body.

As she watched the transfusion go smoothly, Kui Xin's expression eased slightly, but it soon tightened again.

Her eye hurt! More precisely, her left eye hurt, the one with the mechanical prosthetic. At first it had been a faint swelling ache and she'd ignored it, but now the pain was sharp and unmistakable, as if a hard pebble had been crammed into the soft socket.

"Ah!" Kui Xin cried out and clapped a hand over her left eye. Panting, she pulled off her eyepiece and helmet mask, forced her index finger and thumb into the socket and pried out her blue mechanical eye.

Two streams of blood ran from the corner of her eye. Without an eyeball to support it, her eyelid sank in slightly, and then something quickly filled the socket.

Her eye was regenerating!

If she hadn't dug out the mechanical eye, the newly grown eyeball would have pushed it out.

To Kui Xin's relief, her right eye wasn't pushed out.

Both of her original eyes had been removed back when she fled Black Sea City. The dark brown right eye was a lab-grown bionic eye made of cells. Her body hadn't rejected it, so Flesh Regeneration didn't treat it as something "missing" that needed to be repaired.

Click...

The mechanical hand on Kui Xin's left arm came loose. The clasps that had fit snugly against her body no longer lined up. Her severed limb was growing back too, and even the metal skull inside her head was being squeezed by the bone growing around it.

Her head throbbed and the pain seared along her nerves. She couldn't stay upright and dropped to one knee, nearly tearing out the blood-draw tube.

Out of instinct, the doctor moved toward her to help her up, but Kui Xin straightened up on her own and managed to sit back down in the chair. With the hand that was still hooked up to the transfusion line, she grabbed her left arm, detached the mechanical prosthesis and tossed it carelessly on the floor.

"Have you drawn enough blood? Operate on him first," Kui Xin said, gritting her teeth against the pain.

"Not quite enough yet, but if I take any more, your body will..."

Kui Xin cut him off. "Then take more. Give me a nutrient shot, and once it's done, get back to treating him. After that, get ready to open up my skull."

The doctor took a nutrient shot out of the medical kit. Kui Xin took the syringe and injected herself one-handed.

With Flesh Regeneration, she could keep regrowing blood and limbs as long as her body had enough energy. Losing some extra blood was nothing, and regrowing a limb and bone wouldn't kill her either. Amber was a different story. If he didn't get treatment soon, he'd be dead for good.

The doctor took frozen synthetic skin out of the tool kit and put it into a special device to warm it up. Once it was ready, he stitched it onto Amber's mangled body, then used tweezers to pick the bullet fragments out from the cracks in his bone.

The synthetic skin was mainly there to patch the wounds and help them heal. With Amber's constitution as an A-class Awakened, he would recover from these injuries in a few days once the blood loss was dealt with.

"Does it hurt a lot?" Adam asked.

"Hm?" Cold sweat beaded on Kui Xin's forehead. The pain had her so distracted that she hadn't heard what it said at all.

"Nothing," said Adam. "I'm just worried about your condition..."

As time went on, the pain in her head got worse until it drowned out the growing pains and itching in her regenerating left arm. Kui Xin felt like a horse had trampled her skull, or like someone was chiseling it open.

"When that metal skull gets pushed out, it's going to squeeze my head into some weird shape..." Kui Xin thought, trying to find something to laugh about through the pain.

The wait was so excruciating that Kui Xin lost all sense of time.

She had no idea how long it had been when Amber's surgery was finally done.

"Lie down on the bed," the doctor said. "I'll open your skull."

Kui Xin was drenched in cold sweat, like she'd just been fished out of the water, and she practically crawled onto the operating table.

Her Flesh Regeneration was A-rank. A doctor who wasn't skilled enough would never get the metal out.

Kui Xin said quietly, "Be careful how you do this. Peel the skin straight back, take out the metal skull, cut away the overgrowth and stitch me up... Enter a surgical program and let the robotic arms do the whole operation. Don't pick up a scalpel yourself."

The doctor carefully disinfected her and set up the robotic arms.

If the doctor operated by hand, he'd probably no sooner cut her scalp open than his own scalp split open too.

This world's medical technology was very advanced. Minor, trivial operations could be left to machines, but most difficult surgeries were still done by hand. Robotic arms moved rigidly and couldn't adapt to whatever they found inside a patient's skull.

But to keep the doctor from collapsing in a pool of his own blood partway through, Kui Xin would just have to take a bit of a risk.

The surgery began amid searing pain and the whir of the machines.

...

"You're awake."

When Kui Xin opened her eyes, she heard two voices at once.

One was Adam's... the other was Amber's.

"...I'm awake," Kui Xin said hoarsely, her lips barely moving.

Without thinking, she raised a hand, and her fully regenerated left arm went to her ear. Her earpiece had been taken out for the surgery, but at some point it had been put back in.

She traced the curve of her ear up toward the top of her head and felt a whole row of surgical staples holding her scalp together. When she pressed lightly on her head, it felt solid. Her skull had grown back too.

In the quiet hospital room, there was only the sound of two people breathing and the beeping of the vital signs monitor.

Kui Xin turned her head slightly and saw Amber sitting beside the bed.

He was bare from the waist up, with half his body wrapped up like a mummy in bandages. Black stitches crisscrossed the skin that showed.

Amber was clearly recovering well. A little color had returned to his lips, and he had woken up even before Kui Xin. He was peeling an apple, and some packaged instant food sat on the bedside table.

"I put your earpiece back in. I sent the people at this little clinic out to buy some food and plain clothes for a disguise. A lot of the stuff in our gear bags got destroyed in the fighting, so we need to restock." Amber peeled the apple in one unbroken strip. He cut it in half, speared a piece on the tip of the knife and held it out to Kui Xin. "You can get vitamins from medicine, but medicine can't make you feel full the way eating does."

Kui Xin propped herself up in bed, wiped her hands with a disinfectant wipe from beside the bed and took the apple Amber offered.

Suddenly using her original hand again felt a little strange to her, and her movements were slightly awkward.

"Thanks." She bit into the apple.

"I should be thanking you too," Amber said.

When Amber came to, the operation to remove Kui Xin's mechanical skull had been nearly finished. Half the operating table was covered in her blood, and the wound on her head had been horrific. Not even she could have stayed conscious through that.

Flesh Regeneration boosted her ability to survive, but it also caused her a lot of trouble.

"The Resistance could show up any time. We've been here for hours already. We need to leave as soon as we can," Amber said after finishing the apple.

"Yeah, we need to get out of here fast." Kui Xin got out of bed and slowly stretched her arms to get used to her new limb.

Amber might have woken up first, but Kui Xin was in much better shape.

Kui Xin took the clothes to the bathroom and changed. When she came out, Amber was still slowly working his way into his jacket. He couldn't move much, since any sudden movement would split his stitches open.

Kui Xin went into the outer room, got a pair of medical forceps and, using the mirror, pulled the staples out of her head one at a time. She ran her fingers through her hair a couple of times to make sure none were left.

The holes the staples left quickly filled in, and Kui Xin looked exactly as she had before.

She got rid of every trace of her genetic information, down to the last strand of hair. She paid particular attention to the medical equipment and the operating table she had used the night before, checking and cleaning them to make sure nothing was left behind.

"Let's go, Amber." Kui Xin finished her sweep and came back to the ward. Amber had finished dressing and packed everything they needed to take.

"My blood was left back there last night. The Special Intelligence Division might collect it," Amber said.

Kui Xin asked, "Your original is…"

"No idea. Eve would never tell me things like that," Amber said. "It doesn't matter. Whatever the Special Intelligence Division finds won't affect me much. I've never had a normal identity to begin with, so they can't catch me. If anything, Eve will go to a lot of trouble to cover up my identity for me, unless it wants to be exposed."

"Adam." Kui Xin spoke to it through Data Control. "Did last night's mishap cause anything that could hurt us?"

"The Investigation Department is still holding meetings to discuss it. I don't know what the Special Intelligence Division is doing," Adam said. "But you can rest easy on one thing: you wore your helmet and mask the whole time, the injuries you took fighting Augus were minor and healed quickly, and you left no genetic information. All they have is a rough outline of your body, nothing more specific. The gear Amber was carrying was annihilated in the fight with Harriman, and the hover police car you drove was blown to pieces by Matter Annihilation. They can't extract any useful intel."

"So all they can do is guess that I'm Deprivator No. 233." Kui Xin's brow relaxed a little. "Does the Investigation Department suspect you?"

"Not for now," Adam said. "You and I both know full well how arrogant and slow humans can be."

"So now the biggest threat is the Resistance," Kui Xin concluded.

Her eyes darkened a little.

Amber was quick to pick up on the change in her mood. He looked over. "What's wrong?"

"I'm annoyed about what to do with the Resistance's Angel," Kui Xin said. "But before we come up with a plan, we need to rest and get back in shape."

Amber went on, "Then where are we going to rest up, Kui…"

He stopped himself. "Sorry, I forgot I'm supposed to call you by your codename."

"Doesn't matter anymore," Kui Xin said. "Just remember to use codenames when we're on a mission."

Amber paused, startled. "Mm. Okay."

His tone softened, so slightly it was hard to notice.

"You only have a codename. You don't have a name," Kui Xin said suddenly.

"Amber is my codename at Mechanical Dawn, and it's my name too," Amber said. "I used to hate it… but I don't hate it so much now."

"Why?" Kui Xin was surprised. "Eve gave you that name, didn't it?"

"At first, I thought I was just an unlucky insect that had been caught by resin falling from the sky, struggling with all my strength and never breaking free." A faint smile showed on his face. "But resin sinks into the earth and becomes a strange and beautiful gem. I'm not an insect anymore, Kui Xin."

It was the first time Kui Xin had ever seen Amber smile.

Most of the time he was a quiet, silent warrior, and even when he did show emotion, it was rarely anything this positive. What Kui Xin sensed in him most often was conflict, pain, inner struggle, endurance… Now she was seeing something different on his face.

"Angel asked me why I chose you. Maybe from his point of view, we've barely known each other, and he couldn't understand why I'd make that choice… The answer's actually obvious. He just can't figure it out." Amber looked at Kui Xin. "You gave me what they never did. That's the answer."

Kui Xin was taken aback by how blunt Amber was. "To be honest, I didn't expect you to help me so resolutely either."

"Hope is right in front of me, so I have to hold on tight." Amber smiled. "It's better to die holding on to hope than to die trapped in resin. At the very least, I really do have hope."

Hope is the cheapest thing there is, and also the most expensive. Cheap, because it might be nothing but an empty bubble; expensive, because for some people sunk deep in the mire, hope is something they don't even dare to imagine, a luxury forever out of reach.

"So now Amber is a companion we can trust too," Adam said, seemingly with some hidden meaning. "Isn't he?"

Kui Xin didn't answer. She opened a spatial vortex and said, "Let's go."

Amber followed her in.

She held her back straight, but her shoulders sagged slightly, carrying an indescribable ease… as if a little of the invisible burden on them had been taken away.
